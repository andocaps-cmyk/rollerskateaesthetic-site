Do You Need Strong Ankles to Roller Skate?

Written 2 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

No. On inline skates the boot does that job for you. A rigid shell, done up firmly, locks the ankle and turns the wheels into an extension of your leg — the ankle is held, it does not have to hold. The wobble beginners feel on a single line of wheels is fear, not weak ankles. And when an ankle does roll inward, ICP-certified coach Asha Kirkby's answer is that it is alignment, not strength: loose straps, a boot too wide for the foot, or not enough knee bend.

Why do my ankles wobble on roller skates?

On inline skates, almost always because you are scared, not because you are weak. The boot is a rigid shell. When it is fastened properly your ankle is fixed and cannot flop; what you feel as wobble is your body bracing against the fear of standing on one line of wheels. It looks terrifying and feels, once you trust it, shockingly solid. That is why the first fix for wobbly ankles is to do the boot up firmly and bend your knees, not to go and train your ankles.

Are my ankles too weak for inline skates?

Probably not, and the skate was designed so they would not need to be. The whole point of a hard shell is to hold the ankle for you — which is also why you never loosen it to feel better. On skates the ankle carries; off skates it moves. Asha Kirkby, who coaches inline skaters, puts it plainly: speed skaters have powerful legs from deep knee bend, not from isolating ankle muscles.

Why do my ankles roll inward on skates?

Alignment, not strength. If your ankles drop in and you have tightened everything and done exercises and nothing changed, it is because the problem was never muscle. Three causes, from Asha Kirkby: straps that are not actually tight, a boot too wide for your foot so you slide to the inside edge, and knees that are not bent enough to keep your weight over the wheels. Each has its own fix on the pages below, and none of them is an ankle workout.

What about quad skates — do they need stronger ankles?

The question is different on quads, because the boot is softer. Many quad boots are built like a trainer, which is why they are kinder on a beginner's feet early on, and it also means they hold the ankle less than a hard inline shell does. The general rule still stands: do not leave the laces slack. But a quad skater is managing their own ankle more than an inline skater is — which is one reason the inline skate is the easier start.

Should I do ankle exercises before I start skating?

Bend your knees first; that solves more than any exercise. Standing too straight is the beginner fault behind most wobbles and most falls, because it puts your weight behind the wheels. Off the skates, mobility work is useful — on skates the ankle carries, off skates it moves — but it is not a precondition for starting, and no amount of it replaces a properly fastened boot.

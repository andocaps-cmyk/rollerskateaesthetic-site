What Gear Do You Need to Start Roller Skating?

Written 22 August 2026 · updated 29 October 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

To start roller skating you need skates that fit and all four pieces of protection: helmet, wrist guards, knee pads and elbow pads. Then wheels that match your ground — soft outside, hard on a rink. Everything else fits in one hand: a skate tool, spare laces, plasters, a rag and a water bottle.

Which protective gear does a beginner need?

All four pads — they are not a ranked list. Wrist guards take a forward fall, elbow pads are what you land on going backwards, knee pads are what let you drop on purpose instead of fighting the fall, and a helmet covers the one injury you do not get to learn from. A beginner needs every one of them.

Which wheels do you need to start?

Wheels are the cheapest upgrade in skating and the one most people skip. Rink wheels are hard, around 95A and up, because polished floors are smooth. Pavement is not. Soft outdoor wheels, roughly 78A to 85A, absorb the grain instead of rattling through it.

What do you need to maintain roller skates?

The maintenance kit is small enough to be boring. A skate tool, a rag, a bottle of isopropyl alcohol and a bearing lubricant cover every job that keeps skates alive: tightening an axle, cleaning a bearing, wiping a boot, oiling what needs oiling.

What goes in a skate bag?

Mostly the tool. One nut always works loose, spare laces cost nothing, plasters belong permanently in the bag because the heel is the first place a boot rubs, and a rag deals with wheels that pick up grit.

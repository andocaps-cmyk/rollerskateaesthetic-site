How Do You Get Over a Curb on Roller Skates?

Written 2 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Up, you walk it. Down, you do nothing. To get up a curb you do not jump: one push on the left skate, lift the right foot and set it on the curb, push on that right foot, and the left follows — exactly like walking, with a stroke of the skate in between. To come down a curb you stop skating and do nothing at all: knees a little bent, back a little forward, and roll off as if the road simply continued. Jumping is a choice you make later, and not often at the start.

How do you step up a curb on roller skates without jumping?

Like walking, with a push between the steps. Roll towards it at walking pace. A push on the left skate; lift the right foot and put it down on top of the curb; a push on that right foot; the left comes up after it. That is the whole technique, and for a beginner who is afraid of the curb it is honestly the better way — not a workaround until you can jump, but the way to do it.

How do you go down a curb on roller skates?

You stop intervening. Stop pushing, keep rolling, and act as if there were nothing there, as if the road carried on at the same level. Two conditions and only two: knees slightly bent, back slightly curved forward, always. The skates drop to the lower level naturally and you arrive there without having done anything. The technique is not doing something.

Should a beginner jump curbs?

Not much, and not at the start. A jump is yours to decide, which is the whole difference between a jump and a hill: once you have started down a hill you have to see it through, but nobody makes you jump. So you only jump what you are able to jump, and if you try something foolish you have to commit to it like a hill. Early on, walk the curbs up and roll them down. When you do jump, the thing that matters most is simple: knees like springs, no panic, let yourself go.

What if I catch a wheel on the edge?

That is why the knees are bent and the back is forward. Weight over the wheels, slightly forward, is what turns a snag into a stumble you ride out rather than a fall backwards — the fall that hurts. If you do go down, it goes forward onto the pads, which is the fall you have practised. Nothing about the curb changes the beginner rule: body up, knees down.

Is it the same on quads and inline skates?

The walking technique and the do-nothing descent come from inline street skating. On quads outdoors, a certified coach says the fall to watch is backwards, because the wheelbase is short and a bump pitches you back — so the knee bend matters even more.

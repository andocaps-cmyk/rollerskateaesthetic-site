How Tight Should Roller Skates Be?

Written 2 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Tight is about the fastenings, not the size. Laces, straps and buckle go on firmly, so the ankle is held and cannot roll; the size itself comes from the brand's own chart, in millimetres, not from squeezing into a smaller boot. And once they are done up, you do not loosen them to feel better — on inline skates, the held ankle is what makes the skate stable in the first place.

Should roller skates be tight or loose?

Firmly done up, every time. On inline skates the boot is a rigid shell, and when it is fastened properly the ankle is locked and the wheels become an extension of your leg. That is where the stability comes from. There are no four wheels in a rectangle to catch a bad footing, so the ankle has to be held by the boot. A skate you can wiggle in is a skate you cannot steer.

Does “tight” mean buying a smaller size?

No, and this is the mix-up that hurts people. Tight is the laces, straps and buckle. The size is a separate question, and the answer there is to listen to the brand: measure your foot in millimetres, standing, at the end of the day, and read that brand's own chart — including when it tells you to go a size up. The maker knows its own last better than any general rule. Skate sizing is not shoe sizing and it varies wildly from one brand to the next.

Should I loosen my skates if they hurt?

Loosening is never the advice. It is what people do to relieve themselves for a moment, or because they have stopped skating seriously and no longer do anything committed on the skates. If the boot hurts, the cause is almost always one of two things: the wrong size, or a cheap skate that hurts whether it is new or broken in. A good rigid boot still aches a little at first, because rigid is rigid — that is a fact of the sport, not a fault you fix with the laces.

Is it the same on quad skates?

The general rule is the same: do not loosen. Quad boots are often soft, built like a trainer, which is why they are kinder on a beginner's feet early on. But a soft boot is not a reason to leave the laces slack. Done up firmly is the default on both kinds of skate; what changes between them is how much the boot itself holds you, not whether you fasten it.

How do I know they are tight enough?

Two checks, one at a time. First, zero heel lift: rise onto the ball of your foot and the heel should not come up inside the boot. Second, a strap test from ICP-certified inline coach Asha Kirkby: do the top strap up, then bend your knees until they cover your toes. The cuff should hold you at the deepest point of that bend without stopping the movement; if your ankle can still tilt inward down there, the strap is not doing its job yet — and if it will not go tighter, the buckle may be jamming on the strap before the cuff is snug.

The Skating Stride Is Just a Weight Transfer, Sped Up

Written 22 August 2026 · updated 17 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

The skating stride is not a separate skill: it is a weight transfer, sped up. Roll on one foot, bring the skates level, transfer, roll on the other, then do the same thing faster. So the fix for a clumsy stride is never more speed: go back to the slow transfer and clean it up.

Is the skating stride a separate skill?

No. Nobody teaches the skating stride as a separate skill, and there is a good reason for that: it is not one. The stride is a weight transfer you already know, performed faster.

How do you stride on roller skates?

Laid out slowly, it is four things. Roll on one foot. Bring the skates level. Transfer your weight. Roll on the other. Speed those four up until they run into each other and you are striding — there is no fifth ingredient that appears at speed.

How do you fix a clumsy stride?

Not with more speed. A clumsy stride is a clumsy transfer with less time to hide in, so going faster makes it worse and going slower makes it visible.

So when the stride feels wrong, go back to the slow version, find which of the four steps is rushed, and clean that one up. Then speed the whole thing back up.

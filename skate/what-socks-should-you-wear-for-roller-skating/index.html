What Socks Should You Wear for Roller Skating?

Written 2 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Socks that come up past the top of the boot. That is the whole rule. Bare skin against a boot cuff turns into a raw stripe in about twenty minutes, and it is the small thing behind a surprising share of beginner misery. Knee-high is the length that does it on any skate. What a sock will not do is fix a boot that hurts: pain is the fit or the quality of the skate, and the sock only decides whether the cuff rubs on top of that.

Do you need special socks for roller skating?

No — you need the right length. Any sock that clears the top of the boot does the job; what fails is the ankle sock that leaves skin against the cuff. I keep my socks above the boot every time, because the stripe the cuff leaves is the kind of pain you only need once. Beyond length, thickness is a matter of fit: the sock you wear is part of the size you measured, so measure and skate in the same sock.

Should roller skate socks be thick or thin?

Whatever you were wearing when you sized the skate. Skate sizing is done in millimetres against the brand's own chart, and a thick sock added afterwards changes the fit you chose. If you want a thicker sock for warmth or cushioning, size with it on. What does not change with the sock is the rule for the fastenings: done up firmly, so the ankle is held, never loosened to make room.

Will better socks stop my skates hurting?

Only the cuff rub. Not the rest. Most beginner foot pain is fit, not fitness: the wrong size, or a cheap skate that hurts whether it is new or broken in, because of how it is built. A good rigid inline boot still aches a little at first and then stops; a quad boot is often soft like a trainer and kinder early on. A sock can take the rub off the cuff; it cannot change the boot.

Should I bring spare socks and shoes to skate?

Yes to shoes, or at least flip-flops. A pair of light shoes in the bag lets you take the skates off now and then, which matters more on a long session than any sock. The rest of the bag is the tool, spare laces, plasters for the heel, a cloth and water. Spare socks are a comfort, not a rule.

How do I stop my skates and socks smelling?

Wash the socks, air the boot. Liners can be machine-washed cold and must air-dry; the boot itself dries from the inside if you loosen the laces and pull the tongue forward after every session instead of sealing it in a bag.

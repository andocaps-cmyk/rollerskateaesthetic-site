Pick one thing and give it the whole fifteen minutes. Marching, or the duck walk, or plow stops. Not three of them in rotation — one, repeated until the shape stops needing your attention.

Rotation is what makes a routine feel productive without making you better. A movement installs itself by repetition, and four drills in a quarter of an hour give each of them a few minutes: enough to perform, never enough to install. Experienced skaters give a whole session to one move before touching the next, and that is the advice, not a slower version of it.

Come back tomorrow and pick the same thing again. You move on when it feels automatic, not when a timer says so. Fifteen minutes four times a week beats an hour on Sunday, and the session you will actually do beats the one you dread.

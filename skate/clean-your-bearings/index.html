Cleaning a bearing is a simple sequence and there is one trap in it, right at the end. Wheel off, bearing out, shields off, soak in isopropyl alcohol, brush, and dry completely before anything else touches it.

Then: one or two drops of oil, and no more. This is where it goes wrong. More oil does not run smoother. It collects dust, turns into a paste, and leaves the bearing dirtier than it was before you started — so the reward for being generous is doing the whole job again sooner.

You do not need to guess whether you got it right, because a bearing tells you by ear. Clean and correctly oiled, it spins long and it spins silent. Dirty, it sounds like gravel.

Still gravelly after a clean? The clean was not thorough enough — usually the soak was too short or the bearing went back together before it was dry. Do it again rather than adding oil to quieten it, because oil on top of grit is how a bearing gets destroyed instead of serviced.

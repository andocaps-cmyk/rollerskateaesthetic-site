How Do You Stop on Roller Skates?

Written 22 August 2026 · updated 17 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

To stop on roller skates, learn the plow stop first: feet wider than your hips, push your heels apart, toes slightly in, knees bent. Both feet stay on the floor, so it needs no one-legged balance. The T-stop comes second, and on a genuinely steep hill the safest thing you can do is not brake at all.

There is no single brake on roller skates, and that is why the question is hard to answer in one line. There are four ways to lose speed, they suit different situations, and beginners are usually taught them in the wrong order.

How do you stop on roller skates as a beginner?

With the plow stop, the one to learn on day one. Both skates stay down, you widen your stance, and you push your heels away from each other while your toes turn slightly in. Nothing about it requires you to balance on one leg, which is exactly why it works when you are frightened.

How do you do a T-stop on roller skates?

Let the wheels graze, not dig. The T-stop is the drag stop, and it fails for one reason more than any other: too much pressure. Shift your weight onto the supporting leg to lighten the other, set the free skate down behind you, and let those wheels graze the surface. Wheels that dig in stop the skate and not you.

Can you stop with the toe stop?

No: a toe stop is not a brake. Tip onto it and that skate’s wheels leave the ground entirely: it is a fixed point for standing, pivoting and pushing off, not a way to slow down.

A heel brake on inline skates is a real brake, but it wears out, and the bolt underneath it is a deadline rather than a suggestion.

Should you brake going down a hill on roller skates?

Not on a real hill: there, braking is the dangerous move. Once a slope is steep, any gesture that turns a wheel across your direction of travel is more likely to put you on the ground than to slow you. Wheels straight, knees bent, no movement you do not need.

What this page does not cover: how you stop is not the same question as how you slow down safely on a long descent, and it says nothing about stopping on ice or on a skatepark ramp. It also does not tell you which stop to use in traffic — city skating has its own page.

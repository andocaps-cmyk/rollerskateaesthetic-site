Wobbly Ankles Are Almost Never Weak Ankles

Written 31 August 2026 · updated 10 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

No, wobbly ankles on skates are almost never weak ankles. It is an alignment problem: the ankle drops inwards and leaves you sitting on an inside edge. Three things cause it, and none of them is muscle: straps too loose, boots too wide for your foot, and not enough knee bend. Inline technique, from ICP-certified coach Asha Kirkby.

If your ankles collapse inwards on skates, you have almost certainly been told your ankles are weak. You probably tightened everything as far as it would go and started doing ankle strengthening exercises. And nothing changed.

Why do my ankles roll in on skates?

Because it was never a strength problem. It is an alignment problem, and it has a name: the ankle drops inwards and leaves you sitting permanently on an inside edge. A permanent edge is not a wobble you can muscle your way out of — it is a position, and positions are fixed by changing the position.

What causes ankles to collapse inward on skates?

Three things cause it, and none of them is muscle. Ankle straps left too loose, so the boot cannot hold the alignment it was designed to hold. Boots that are simply too wide for your foot, which leaves the foot room to roll inside the shell. And not enough knee bend, or a body position that puts the weight in the wrong place.

Do ankle exercises fix wobbly ankles?

No. Speed skaters have powerful legs. They did not get them by isolating ankle muscles — they got them from a deep knee bend held for hours and correct alignment through the whole leg. The ankle is downstream of the knee, not the other way round.

How do you fix wobbly ankles on skates?

Work in this order. Tighten the straps properly. Check whether the boot is too wide for your foot, because if it is, no amount of technique will fix it. Then work on the knee bend, which is the part that takes actual time.

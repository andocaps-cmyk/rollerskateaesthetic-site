How Do You Skate Backwards on Roller Skates?

Written 22 August 2026 · updated 29 October 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

At a wall, push your heels apart, then back together: the plow-stop shape run in reverse, and it rolls you backwards. Look over one shoulder rather than straight down. Keep your weight on the front of your skates and your knees pressed together, which is genuinely hard work. It takes most people about three sessions.

How long does it take to learn to skate backwards?

About three sessions, and backwards is where most people quit. Start against a wall, facing forwards, and work the heels-apart, heels-together shape until it rolls you backwards on its own. It is the plow stop you already know, inverted.

Why do you fall the first time you skate backwards?

Everyone leans back the first time, and everyone falls. Rolling backwards keeps the pressure on the front of your skates, exactly as it does going forwards. That is the part nobody expects, and correcting it removes most of the early falls in one go.

What is the hardest part of skating backwards?

The hardest part has nothing to do with your feet. Your knees have to stay pressed together, and holding them there is tiring — which is why most people give up on the position before they give up on the movement. Knowing that it is supposed to be hard work is half of getting through it.

Where should you look when skating backwards?

Over your shoulder, not at the floor. Looking down drops your weight backwards, which is the one thing you are trying not to do, and it hides whatever you are rolling towards.

What this page does not cover: this is the first backwards roll and the position it needs. It does not cover backwards crossovers, transitions between forwards and backwards, or stopping while rolling backwards.

Is It Worth Putting New Wheels on Cheap Roller Skates?

Written 2 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

It depends on how, and how often, you skate. If it is a stroll now and then, yes, it is worth it: a change of wheels is the one upgrade that makes the same skates feel like different skates. If you skate a lot, then at some point the money should go into a proper skate rather than into dressing up a cheap one — because a cheap boot hurts by construction, and no wheel fixes a boot.

Will new wheels make cheap skates feel better?

Outdoors, yes, more than anything else you can bolt on. Tarmac has grain, and a hard wheel sends every bit of it straight into your feet; a softer wheel deforms a little and absorbs it. That is why the same skates feel awful outside on hard wheels and fine on soft ones. For pavement, the range that works is about 78A to 85A; for a smooth rink floor it is 95A and up, where a hard wheel is faster because the floor is already smooth. The number is printed on the wheel, and higher is harder.

When is it worth it, and when is it not?

Worth it: occasional skating, for the pleasure of it. If you take the skates out now and then for a stroll, new wheels are a cheap, real improvement and the skates will do what you need. Not worth it, or not for long: if you skate often. Then I think you have to start investing in the skate itself at some point, because what will fail you is not the wheels.

Will new wheels fix skates that hurt?

No. Pain is the boot, and the boot is where cheap shows. A low-quality skate hurts whether it is new or broken in — the pain comes from how it is built, not from its age, and no amount of wearing it or re-wheeling it changes that. Good skates still ache a little at first, because a rigid boot is rigid, but then they stop. If your cheap skates hurt, the answer is a better skate at the right size, not better wheels.

Do I need new bearings too?

Usually not, and the number on the box means nothing. The ABEC rating is a machining tolerance written for industrial spindles at speeds no skater reaches; a high number on cheap steel with bad seals rolls worse than a modest bearing that is well made. What changes rolling is keeping dirt out and re-oiling. A clean ABEC 5 beats a filthy ABEC 9 every time. Wipe the wheels after a session outside, and open the bearings when they start to sound dry.

What wheels should I buy for cheap skates used outside?

Soft ones, in the 78A-85A band, in the size your skate takes. Check the wheel size your plate or frame accepts before buying. The outdoor wheels guide below lists what named skaters ride outside, with their reasons; the two sets at the end of this page are from it.

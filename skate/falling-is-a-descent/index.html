You are not trying to avoid the fall. You are trying to slow it down. That is a different goal and it changes what you practise: a good fall is a descent, not a drop.

It has three movements, and they come from Najete Kada's Tuto roller series. From the safety position — knees bent, hands resting on your knees — lower yourself slowly into a squat until you touch the floor. Put your hands on the floor. Then stretch your legs out in front of you.

She takes it slowly, every time, and that is the point: in her lesson, learning to fall means slowing the fall right down. The safety position is also what she tells you to get into the moment you lose your balance while rolling, because it makes you compact and brings you closer to the floor.

Practise the three movements before you need them, the way she repeats them in the video. Do it slowly, in full protective gear, on a soft floor such as grass or a mat, and never alone. It is a drill for the slow falls every beginner takes; it promises nothing about a fall at speed. Quad technique, from Najete Kada's Tuto roller series.

Getting back up has a shape, and flailing is not it. The shape is the one a knight takes to kneel: one knee down, one skate flat on the floor in front of you.

How do you get up after falling on roller skates?

In order: roll over onto your knees so you are facing the floor. Plant one skate flat in front of you. Then push up off that front leg while your hands press down on the knee to help. The hands are not decoration — they turn a leg press into something your arms share.

Why must the skate go down flat?

The skate goes down flat, all wheels touching. On a toe it rolls out from under you; on a heel it does the same in the other direction. Flat is the only position that stays where you put it while you load it.

Why learn a slow way to get up?

It works when you are tired, which is exactly when you will need it. That is the whole argument for learning a slow, repeatable method instead of scrambling: scrambling works on the first fall of the session and not on the sixth.

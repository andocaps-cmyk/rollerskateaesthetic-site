Skaters who ride real descents describe it in physical terms, not poetic ones: adrenaline arriving straight in the chest, like a needle that does not stop for the whole length of the hill.

What separates it from every other fast thing is how ridiculous the support is. You are not on a bike. Not on a board. Not on a scooter. There is nothing under you but wheels, under a trainer or under a shell, and the whole machine is absurdly small next to your body.

A fairground ride always keeps a little safety in reserve. This keeps none, and that is the difference people are actually describing when they say nothing else compares.

It only arrives whole under the right conditions: a level of skill already there, a good road, and no danger other than falling — no traffic, nothing unpredictable. Then you can stop managing the situation and take the whole thing.

Why Do My Feet Hurt When I Roller Skate?

Written 22 August 2026 · updated 29 October 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Your feet usually hurt because of fit, not fitness: the wrong size, or a cheap skate that hurts whether it is new or broken in. Good skates still ache at first, because a rigid inline boot is rigid. Measure your foot in millimetres, keep your socks above the cuff, and do not loosen the laces.

Will roller skates stop hurting once they are broken in?

A good skate will; a bad one will not. The advice you will hear most often is to keep skating until the boot breaks in. That is true of a good skate and false of a bad one. A cheap skate hurts because of how it is built, and no amount of wearing it changes that. Quads are often made soft like a trainer, which makes them kinder early on; a hard inline boot is unforgiving at first and then stops being a problem.

Is the size the problem?

Sizing is where most of the pain actually comes from. Skate sizing is not shoe sizing. Measure your foot in millimetres, read the brand's own chart rather than converting, and buy snug. A loose boot lets your foot move inside it, and a foot that moves is a foot that rubs.

What else makes roller skates hurt?

Two small things cause a surprising share of the misery. Socks have to clear the top of the boot: bare skin against a cuff becomes a raw stripe in about twenty minutes. And when skates hurt, the instinct is to open the laces — on inline skates that is the wrong move, because the held ankle is what makes the skate stable in the first place.

What if the problem is heat, smell or damp?

Then the answer is care rather than fit. Liners can be machine-washed cold and must then air-dry, and a boot that gets loosened and opened after every session dries from the inside instead of staying damp.

What this page does not cover: this page is about pain that comes from the boot, the fit and the socks. It is not medical advice, and it does not cover injury, swelling that lasts after you take the skates off, or pain in the knee, hip or back. If pain persists off the skate, see someone qualified.

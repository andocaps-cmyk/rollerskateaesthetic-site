Why Do I Keep Falling Backwards on Roller Skates?

Written 2 November 2026 · updated 13 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Almost every backward fall has one cause: your weight is behind your wheels. Standing up straight puts it there. Fighting to stay upright when you wobble puts it there. Looking down at the ground, especially when you try to roll backwards, puts it there. The fix is the same each time: bend your knees more than feels natural, keep your body up and your knees down, and when you do go, go forward onto the pads — which is why elbow pads, the piece that takes the backward fall, are not optional at the start.

Why does standing straight make me fall backwards?

Because on wheels, upright means behind. Your feet can roll out from under you, so the only safe place for your weight is over the wheels, slightly forward. Stand tall like you would on foot and your hips sit behind the skates; the first push sends the wheels forward and you go down the other way. Bend your knees more than you think you need to — it always looks like too much from the inside and never is.

Why do I fall backwards when I wobble?

Because fighting the fall is what tips you back. The reflex when you lose balance is to straighten up and resist, and straightening up is precisely the movement that puts your weight behind the wheels. The basic advice is the opposite: descend forward, knees, then pads. Practise it on carpet or grass, in full gear, from standing, and never alone. Keep it slow: the point is to have been through a few falls at walking pace before you ever fall at speed. No practice makes a fall at speed safe.

Why do I fall backwards when I try to skate backwards?

You are looking at the ground. Going backwards, the instinct is to look down at your feet, and looking down drops your head and puts your weight behind the wheels — the same fall again. Look over one shoulder instead, never at the floor. The push comes from the heels: spread them, bring them together, the plough stop run in reverse. Weight still forward, even though you are travelling back.

Is it because my skates are unstable?

On inline skates, almost certainly not. A rigid inline boot locks your ankle and the wheels become an extension of your leg; the wobble beginners feel is fear, not the skate. On quads, the boot is softer and the fall to watch outdoors is still the backward one — a quad heel is already raised, and you bend your knees anyway. On either skate, the question to ask is not what the skate is doing but where your weight is.

How do I protect myself from a backward fall?

Elbow pads, then knees that are already bent. Going backwards, your elbows reach the ground first — that is what you catch yourself on, which is why they are as important as every other pad when you start. Padded shorts cover the tailbone. And the fall that you have practised forward is the one that stops happening backward: knee pads are what let you choose to drop instead of fighting.

What this page does not cover: this page is about the beginner backward fall on flat ground. It does not cover falls on hills, on wet ground, or on a ramp, and it is not medical advice for a fall that has already happened.

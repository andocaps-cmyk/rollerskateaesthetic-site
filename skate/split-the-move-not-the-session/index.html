Split the Move, Not the Session

Written 28 September 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

A skating session is built around one move, not a menu of moves: a whole session per move before the next one. ICP-certified coach Asha Kirkby structures inline practice the same way from the inside: she splits a move into its components, trains each one separately, then puts the pieces back together. The parts change through the hour; the move does not.

There is a way of planning a skating session that looks productive and teaches almost nothing: a little marching, a little gliding, a stop, a turn, all in an hour. This site has said before that a session is built the other way round.

One move per session. A whole session on it before the next one. That is not a comfort rule, it is what makes a move stick, and it rules out every plan that stacks moves like a menu.

A certified coach structures inline practice the same way, from the inside of the move. She splits one move into its components, gives each component its own drill, trains them separately, and only then puts the pieces back together. The parts change through the hour; the move does not.

So the two ideas are one idea. Split the move, not the session: the session stays on one thing, and the variety you need comes from taking that one thing apart. The next pages take the stride apart as the worked example.

The one-move-per-session rule at the top of this page is the site's own skater's, who skates inline. The component drills are from Asha Kirkby of Skatefresh, an instructor certified by the Inline Certification Program; she teaches inline skating, and that is the discipline this page is about.

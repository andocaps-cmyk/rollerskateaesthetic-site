The Powerslide Takes Months of Practice, Not Sessions

There is a gap between being able to do a move and being able to use it, and on the powerslide that gap is measured in months.

It is learnable, including from a forward transition entry. That is the encouraging half: you do not need to already be an advanced skater to start, and the entry most people are afraid of is a normal part of the progression.

But a certified coach is blunt about the timescale: several months of conscious practice to build the entry speed up to something useful in a real situation. Conscious is the operative word — repetition without attention does not compound.

Knowing the shape is not owning it at speed. The version you can do at walking pace and the version that saves you at the bottom of a hill are the same movement with a year of difference between them, and only one of them is a stop.

One thing sits above all of this, and it is mine rather than hers: a hill I doubt I can stop on, I ride it out rather than brake. Trying to brake halfway down a gradient you have already misjudged is worse than riding it to the flat — Cass, a non-professional street skater.

Technique from Asha Kirkby of Skatefresh, an instructor certified by the Inline Certification Program. She teaches inline skating, and that is the discipline this page is about.

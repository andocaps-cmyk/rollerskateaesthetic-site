Can You Switch From Inline Skates to Quad Skates?

Written 2 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Yes, but the switch is not symmetrical. If you start on quads, moving to inline skates later is easy. If you start on inline skates, you have to relearn almost everything to skate quads. So there are two honest answers to “which should I start on”: if the question is the easiest start, regardless of model, it is inline. If the question is the start that keeps every door open, it is quads.

Is it easier to go from quads to inline, or inline to quads?

Quads to inline is the easy direction. Someone who learned on quads can pick up inline skates without starting over. The other way round, an inline skater who tries quads is back at the beginning on most things. That is my experience and it is the whole answer to the question in the title; everything below is about what to do with it.

Which should I start on if I just want the easiest start?

Inline. An inline boot is a rigid shell: it locks your ankle, and the wheels become an extension of your leg, so the skate is more stable than it looks and more stable than a quad — at a standstill as much as rolling. The wobble beginners feel on a single line of wheels is fear, not the skate. For a start that is simply easier, whatever the model, inline wins.

Which should I start on if I want to keep my options open?

Quads. Because the skills carry one way and not the other, starting on quads is the start that leaves the inline door open for later. Quads also keep what they are good at: they turn tighter, they suit the rink and dancing, and their boots are often soft like a trainer, which is kinder on a beginner's feet early on. None of that is stability — it is manoeuvrability, and the two get confused constantly.

Why is the switch so lopsided?

I can tell you that it is; I will not dress up a guess as the reason. Starting on quads, moving to inline is easy. Starting on inline, you relearn everything on quads. That is what I have seen, and it is the part to plan around: if you start inline, budget for being a beginner again the day you try quads.

Do I need different protective gear to switch?

No — the same four, and all of them again. When you switch, you are a beginner again on that skate, which is exactly when nothing is optional: wrist guards for the forward fall, elbow pads for the backward one, knee pads, helmet.

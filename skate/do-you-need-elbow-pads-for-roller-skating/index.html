Do You Need Elbow Pads for Roller Skating?

Written 2 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Yes — when you are starting out, you wear them. Elbow pads are the piece people skip, and they are the piece you land on in a backward fall, the one fall nobody plans for. At the beginning every protection matters, with no ranking between them: wrist guards for the forward fall, elbow pads for the backward one, knee pads so you can choose to go down, a helmet. Later, once your skating is settled, dropping a piece becomes your call and your responsibility.

Why do beginners skip elbow pads?

Because the fall they picture is forward. Everyone imagines tripping and landing on their hands and knees, so wrist guards and knee pads feel obvious and elbow pads feel optional. But a beginner's most common fall goes the other way: standing too straight puts your weight behind the wheels, the skates shoot forward, and you go down backwards. Nobody expects that fall, and that is exactly the one the elbows take.

What do elbow pads actually protect?

They are what you catch yourself on going backwards. When you tip back, your elbows reach the ground before anything else — before your tailbone or the back of your head, which is where a backward fall hurts. Wrist guards are for the forward fall — the reflex of putting a bare palm down at speed is faster than any decision, and it is what breaks wrists. Knee pads do something different again: they let you drop forward on purpose instead of fighting to stay up, and a chosen fall stops a wobble before it becomes a real crash.

Which protective gear is most important for roller skating?

At the start, none of them is more important than the others. Any list has to be written in some order, and that order says nothing about priority. The honest version is this: when you are a beginner, the advice is to leave none of them out. After that, it is your responsibility if you decide not to. Ranking them, or calling one piece optional on day one, is the mistake — and I have seen it written on plenty of sites.

Can I stop wearing elbow pads once I am good?

That is a decision you earn later, not one you make at the start. Once your skating is settled, you can change the order or take a piece off, knowing what you are giving up. For me the honest line is: tight pads at the start, every one of them, and then it is on you. What this page will not do is tell you which one to drop first.

Do I need elbow pads for inline skates too, or just quads?

Both. The backward fall is a beginner fall, not a quad fall. On inline skates a rigid boot holds your ankle and the skate is more stable than it looks, but a held ankle does nothing for weight that sits behind the wheels. The four-pad rule is the same whichever skate you start on.

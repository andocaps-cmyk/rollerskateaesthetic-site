New Gear Will Not Skate for You

There are thousands of videos about boots, frames, wheels, wheel sizes and frame positions. Most of them are honest and informative. Together they still teach one thing that is false: that the next piece of equipment is what stands between you and the skating you want.

Here is a test you can run yourself, without believing anybody. Does every skater running a slightly rockered frame produce beautiful flowing curves? Clearly not. You have seen plenty who do not. The frame helps a skater achieve those curves — it does not produce them, and no arrangement of hardware ever has.

That is the general shape of it. Equipment lets a developed technique express itself more fully. It cannot manufacture a technique that is not there yet, so a change of gear made before the technique exists mostly buys you a different set of frustrations.

What you can legitimately ask of your gear is comfort and suitability. It should not hurt, and it should match the kind of skating you actually do most, rather than the kind you imagine doing. Those two things are worth spending on.

Everything past that is progressions and practice, worked through one stage at a time. It is slower to hear and considerably faster to do.

How Do You Learn to Roller Skate as a Beginner?

Written 22 August 2026 · updated 10 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

To learn to roller skate as a beginner, go in order: march on the spot, then duck walk in a V stance, then glide. Learn to get up and to plow stop early, because you will use both constantly. Practise one thing per session, on flat, smooth, empty ground such as an indoor rink or an empty tennis court.

What do beginners learn first on roller skates?

The first three moves come before gliding, and skipping them is what causes the day-one backwards fall. March on the spot with bent knees to learn that the skates hold you. Then duck walk: toes out in a V, small steps, still no rolling. Only then let the skates roll.

How do you learn to stride on skates?

The stride people think they need to learn separately is not a separate skill. Roll on one foot, bring both skates level with each other, set the returning skate down underneath your hips, transfer your weight, roll on the other. Speed that sequence up and you are striding. The order is what matters; the speed arrives on its own.

How long should a beginner practise each move?

Give a short session to one thing. Fifteen minutes is not enough time for four drills, and that is exactly why it works: spend the whole session on marching, or on the duck walk, or on plow stops, until the shape stops needing your attention. Movements install themselves by repetition, not by rotation.

Where should a beginner learn to roller skate?

Where you practise decides how fast you progress. Flat and smooth beats everything else. Slopes, gravel and crowds all punish the exact wobble you are trying to get rid of, so they are worth avoiding entirely while you learn.

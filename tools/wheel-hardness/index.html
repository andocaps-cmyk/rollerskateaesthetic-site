Skate Wheel Hardness Guide

Written 7 October 2026 · updated 29 October 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Which wheel hardness do you need? Outside on asphalt, go soft: 78A to 85A. On a smooth rink floor, go hard: 95A and up. Hardness, also called durometer, is printed on the wheel as a number followed by an A; the higher the number, the harder the wheel. A soft wheel absorbs the grain of rough ground.

Why does wheel hardness matter?

A rink floor is already smooth, so a hard wheel rolls faster there. Asphalt has grain, and a hard wheel sends every bit of it up into your feet; a soft wheel gives a little and absorbs it. That is why the same skates feel like different skates after a wheel change.

What wheel size do you need?

For quads, outdoor wheels are usually around 62 mm, rink wheels around 57 to 58 mm, and park wheels smaller. For inline, fitness and urban skates use 76 to 90 mm, distance skates 100 to 125 mm, and aggressive skates 55 to 72 mm. Your frame or plate limits the size you can fit: check your skate before buying.

FAQ

What hardness are outdoor roller skate wheels?

78A to 85A. Riedell gives the same range for outdoor wheels.

What hardness for an indoor rink?

95A and up. Riedell's range for indoor wheels starts lower, at 88A, and goes to 101A.

Does my weight change the hardness?

A little: heavier skaters can use harder wheels, lighter skaters softer ones, within the range for the surface. The surface comes first.

Can I use outdoor wheels indoors?

You can, but outdoor wheels feel slow on a rink, and indoor wheels lack grip outside.

What hardness for roller derby?

It depends on the floor. Riedell gives 88A-95A on sport court and wood, 84A-88A on slick or polished concrete, and 84A-90A on sealed or coated floors; Atom's matrix also moves with the skater's weight. Derby wheels are commonly 59 mm tall and 38 mm wide (Riedell's Radar Halo, for example).

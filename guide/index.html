What Top Skaters Recommend for Beginners: Gear Advice, Compiled

Compiled 7 October 2026 · updated 24 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

This page gathers what recognised skaters say about gear — world champions, team pros, coaches and a few industry figures — discipline by discipline, with a link to every source. On 7 October 2026 we profiled 93 skaters, coaches and industry figures across 5 discipline groups, from 156 source links (team pages, interviews, articles and 47 YouTube videos); many of them turned out to say nothing about gear, and we say so below.

It is a compilation, not a review: we have not tested the skates. Where skaters agree, we count them. Where they disagree, we show both sides. Where nobody has said anything, we leave the gap visible.

What almost every top skater agrees on

These points come up across disciplines. The count is the number of named people in our files who say or show it; a skater who stays silent on a point is not counted for or against.

1. Protective gear, every piece — 29 people

Derby and park: Bonnie Thunders, Scald Eagle, Atomatrix, Swede Hurt, Snot Rocket Science, Satan's Little Helper, Nina Erwes, Jackie Daniels, Carolina “Caro” Hernandez, Tia Pitman, Slam, Michelle Steilen (Estro Jen). Rink and dance: Ana Coto, Marician Dedeaux Brown, Oumi Janta, Kelly (Skate Base London). Inline: Asha (Skatefresh), Jan Welch, Alex Shulgan, Dustin Werbeski, Danny Aldridge, Klaudia (Klaudia & Michał), Chloé Seyrès, Bobi Spassov, Ricardo Lino. Downhill: Etienne Herreros, Gabe Holm, Noel Jarillo, Alexandre Lebrun.

Estro Jen gives the order: helmet, then wrist guards (the most common skating injury, she says), then knees, then elbows (video, 9 Jan 2021). The skater behind this site gives no order for a beginner: every piece matters, and you leave none of them out. Bobi Spassov wore full pads, shin guards included, for his first six years of aggressive skating (video, 12 May 2024).

2. A boot that supports the ankle — 13 people

Asha (Skatefresh), Alex Shulgan, Jan Welch, Casper (Powerslide) for inline; Lisa J, Caro Hernandez, Kid Ace, Tia Pitman and Dirty Deborah Harry for quad; Viktor Thorup, Bill Begg and Alexandre Lebrun for speed and downhill; Niko Salaman for aggressive. Dirty Deborah Harry adds that ankle support exists in entry-level skates too (video, 21 Jun 2022).

3. Technique before gear — 12 people

Suzy Hotrod, Atomatrix, Smarty Pants, Swede Hurt, Jackie Daniels, Asha (Skatefresh), Casper (Powerslide), Klaudia, Joey Mantia, Ewen Fernandez, Felix Rijhnen, Viktor Thorup. Suzy Hotrod put it this way: the same gear will not make you as good, “practicing hard will” (WFTDA, Nov 2011).

4. Fit before brand — 11 people

Dominic Bruce, Andrew DiPaolo, Hazen Bell, Gabriel Talamantes, Chemi Simiyu, Nick Lomax (aggressive and urban); Casper, Klaudia, Jan Welch (inline); Dirty Deborah Harry (quad); Viktor Thorup (speed). Inline sources want it snug — heel locked, toes may touch (Casper, video, 11 Mar 2024). Dominic Bruce blames a lasting ankle injury on a boot that did not suit his foot (Be-Mag, 2 Oct 2018).

5. Wheel hardness follows the surface — 10 people

Suzy Hotrod, Scald Eagle, Estro Jen, Caro Hernandez, Lisa J, Dirty Deborah Harry, Kim Manning, Casper, Viktor Thorup, Gabriel Talamantes. The pattern: softer wheels outdoors, on rough ground and in the rain; harder wheels on smooth rink floors and in skateparks. Dirty Deborah Harry: you can keep the same skates and just change the wheels.

6. Avoid the very cheapest — 4 people

Oumi Janta (very cheap skates wear out and are risky for dancing), Dunbeezy (look for a leather boot, an aluminium plate and an adjustable toe stop), Lisa J (metal plate, never plastic), Viktor Thorup (real urethane wheels, not plastic).

Quad: rink, jam and dance

For a beginner, without brands: a supportive boot with easy-to-adjust lacing, 62–65 mm wheels and full pads, and try before you buy (Kelly, instructor at Skate Base London, 9 Sep 2024). Hard wheels indoors, a separate softer set outdoors. A cheap skate worth buying has a leather boot, an aluminium plate and an adjustable stop (Dunbeezy).

  • Seven World Skate champions in artistic and dance — Luca Lucaroni (world champion 2016–2019), Rebecca Tarlazzi (world champion 2019), Pau Garcia Domeç (world champion 2021), Natalia Baldizzone Morales (solo dance world champion 2021), Asya Sofia Testoni and Giovanni Piccolantonio, Ana and Pedro Walgode, Llorenç Alvarez Caballero — all skate a high, stiff artistic boot. Sponsor: Edea, all seven Their plates and wheels are not published. Edea team pages, undated, read 7 Oct 2026.
  • Kim Manning, presented as a professional roller skater (record not verified): high leather artistic boot, artistic plate, 96A indoor wheels and separate outdoor wheels. Listed in the video description, not spoken. video, 11 May 2020
  • Dunbeezy, rhythm skating teacher on YouTube (reputation outside YouTube not verified): boot height, stop and wheels depend on the regional style — Chicago/JB high boot, jam plugs, hard wheels; Detroit high leather boot, small hard slick wheels, full toe stop; Atlanta low speed boot, wide wheels. No sponsor mentioned. video, 12 Aug 2024
  • Oumi Janta, Berlin jam skater behind the 2020 revival videos: no gear specs; learn the stance first (knees bent, weight forward), learn to get up, wear pads if you feel unsafe. No skate sponsor verified. video, 20 Sep 2020; Kaltblut, 29 Feb 2020
  • Ana Coto, whose 2020 dance videos started a buying wave: wear protection — she broke her wrist early on. Paper, 11 Mar 2020 Marician Dedeaux Brown: learn to fall rather than trying not to. BuzzFeed News, 2020

Disagreement: beginner wheel size — 62–65 mm (Kelly) versus small wheels for rhythm gliding (Dunbeezy, no figure given). High boot versus low speed boot for jam depends on the style.

Quad: park, roller derby and outdoor

For a beginner, without brands: soft wheels outdoors (Caro Hernandez, Lisa J, Dirty Deborah Harry); hard wheels from day one at the skatepark, 92A or harder, and 62 mm rather than 57 mm while you learn to pump (Estro Jen); a firm, padded boot and a metal plate, never plastic (Lisa J); an adjustable toe stop rather than a fixed “bullseye” one, and a boot that fits like a slipper (Dirty Deborah Harry).

  • Michelle Steilen (Estro Jen), founder of Moxi and a leading park quad skater: full pads, helmet first (her order; this site gives none for a beginner); hard park wheels from the start. Sponsor: own brand Moxi; signature RollerBones wheel She recommends her own wheel. video, 9 Jan 2021; 57 vs 62 mm video, 31 Jan 2021
  • Carolina “Caro” Hernandez, Colombian bowl skater, ex-derby: stiff boot with a heel for ramps, short toe stop, very hard 103A wheels in the park; soft wheels on the street, easier on the knees. Sponsor: Moxi video, 7 Oct 2020
  • Kid Ace, Moxi team rider (record not verified): a supportive boot for hard landings; a longer plate felt more stable. Sponsor: Moxi video, 25 Sep 2020
  • Tia Pitman, winner of the Concrete Waves invitational (2023): firmer boot for ankle support. Sponsor: Chuffed, S1 S1, 3 Nov 2023
  • Dirty Deborah Harry, coach (Dirty School of Skate): wheels can be swapped, so any pair will do to start; for derby, little or no heel. Says she supports no brand. video, 21 Jun 2022
  • Derby champions on WFTDA profiles (2010–2016): Bonnie Thunders (four World Cups, 8 WFTDA titles), Scald Eagle (WFTDA champion 2015, 2016), Suzy Hotrod, Jackie Daniels (93–95A wheels), Swede Hurt, Snot Rocket Science, Juniper Simonis, Satan's Little Helper, Nina Erwes, Atomatrix, Smarty Pants. All on aluminium competition plates; full pads. Gear may be sponsored without the profile saying so Bonnie Thunders, Aug 2010; Scald Eagle, Oct 2015; Jackie Daniels, Mar 2010
  • Scald Eagle via a Bont blog post: choose by hardness, not colour; 88–92A on polished concrete, 95–98A on grippy wood. Sponsor: Bont (not formally verified) Exact attribution of the advice is not verified. Bont, 14 Sep 2020
  • Lisa J (Boardwalk Skater, 35 years skating by her own account): the boot is the most important part. 25 Apr 2023

Disagreement: leather boots (Thunders, Hotrod, Daniels, Simonis) versus carbon (Scald Eagle, Snot Rocket Science, Satan's Little Helper, Swede Hurt) in derby; heel for ramps (Caro) versus little heel for derby (Dirty).

Inline: urban, fitness and slalom

For a beginner, without brands: a rigid or clearly supportive boot; a snug fit; four wheels around 80 mm, 90 mm at most; 80–88A; keep the heel brake until you master other stops (Asha; the skater behind this site, a non-professional street skater, found it too weak to stop in time and took it off once trained: see the heel brake: two honest opinions); a removable frame (165 mm standard or Trinity mount) so you can change wheels later. Sources: Asha of Skatefresh (2 Feb 2021), Jan Welch (14 Apr 2026), Alex Shulgan (NBC, 9 Sep 2024), Casper of Powerslide (11 Mar 2024).

  • Asha (Skatefresh), coach at a leading UK school: beginners in soft boots are “twice as likely to pronate”. Later: pick wheel size for the skating you will do most; technique matters more than wheels or frame. No declared sponsor; skates Powerslide. 2021; video, 21 Dec 2022
  • Jan Welch, editor of Big Wheel Blading, 30 years in the industry: 80 mm, 80–85A, measure your foot in Mondopoint. Owns Dream Urethane (wheels)
  • Alex Shulgan, professional skater: rigid boot and four wheels to start; a skate helmet rather than a bike one. Co-owner of In Move Skates
  • Casper, presenter of Powerslide's channel (industry figure, not a competitor): hard boot if your ankles collapse inward; choose the skate for the boot, not the wheels; keep the stock bearings. Powerslide
  • Ricardo Lino, pro skater since the late 1990s: support comes from the shell and cuff height, not the “hard” or “soft” label. video, 2 Aug 2017
  • Klaudia (Klaudia & Michał, says she is a world champion; link to Klaudia Hartmanis not verified): learn the first slalom tricks on any skates; never too big; full pads. She recommends FR skates because the frame switches from flat to rockered in minutes. video, 3 Mar 2025
  • Slalom champions Lorenzo Guslandi (3× pair slalom world champion), Lorenzo Demuru (battle world champion 2018), Miriam Fatmi García, Enrique Rubio (slide world champion 2018), Kim Sung Jin (Seba): short rockered frames, 76–90 mm wheels around 85–86A. Sponsors: Powerslide, Seba Powerslide team pages, undated
  • Fitness and distance: Judit Abarca (creator of Rollerobics) on 3 × 100 mm, Sheila Juan Franco (marathon, 24h Rollertour) on 3 × 125 mm. Sponsor: Powerslide Off-road: Dustin Werbeski — start on grass or packed dirt, learn to stop, wear a helmet (27 Jul 2017). Urban: Danny Aldridge (Rollerblade) — basics first, then full pads (22 Oct 2018).

Disagreement: three big wheels for a beginner (see below); soft boots (Asha against, Welch and Casper accept them).

Inline: aggressive

For a beginner, without brands: learn to skate before buying aggressive skates — they are not made for distance; full pads including shin guards for learning grinds; what makes an aggressive skate is the soul plate and frame; anti-rocker is easier for grinding, flat (four equal wheels) easier to turn — try both (Bobi Spassov and Ricardo Lino, 12 May 2024). Most setups we found use 56–64 mm wheels at 88–92A.

  • Bobi Spassov, pro with Roces signature models. Sponsor: Roces
  • Winterclash podium riders on Be-Mag's “Blade Check”: Antony Pottier (pro winner 2018, Seba), Dominic Bruce (2nd, 2019, Razors, Ground Control) — pick the boot for your foot shape, Fred Bukowski (am champion 2015, Seba) — one flat setup for everything, Tomek Przybylik (USD) — flat with big wheels. Pottier, 19 Jun 2016; Bruce, 2 Oct 2018; Bukowski, 27 Dec 2018
  • Chris Haffey (X Games gold 2009, 2011): experiment with the boot settings — he cuts a V in the cuff. Sponsor: Remz video, 8 Oct 2008
  • Jon Julio, founder of Them Skates: an adjustable cuff, lower or higher for more or less forward flex. Own brand video, 27 Aug 2019
  • Also profiled: Si Coburn (keep screws tight), Niko Salaman (rigid boot, low frame), Gabriel Talamantes (softer wheels in park, harder on the street), Bryce Orfield (hardest wheels for durability), Sam Crofts, Hazen Bell, Andrew DiPaolo (wide feet), Stephen Babcock, Alex Burston — all Be-Mag Blade Check, all sponsored.

Inline: speed and downhill

For a beginner, without brands: four wheels of 100 mm for an adult starting speed skating, or a 4 × 110 frame to grow into; real urethane wheels, softer on rough roads and in the rain; ABEC 7 or 9 bearings are enough; move to a low speed boot only when ankles and technique are ready (Viktor Thorup, two-time Olympian on ice, 25 Jul 2023). Thorup: “I suggest 100 millimeters for adult beginners.” Rollerblade World Team; owns a skate shop

  • Bill Begg, coach: moving to 4 × 100 “should not be intimidating”; speed boots tire the ankles, plan a transition. Works for X-Tech Inline Planet, 30 Jan 2013
  • Joey Mantia, world champion on inline and ice: no magic move, only attention to basics; thinks anybody can step on 3 × 125 mm wheels. Bont, Powerslide, Cadomotus at different times video, 6 Jul 2015
  • Bart Swings (five straight Berlin marathon wins by 2017), Felix Rijhnen (world champion 2017), Sandrine Tas, Ewen Fernandez (prefers soft wheels): sponsor setups on 110–125 mm wheels, no gear advice for beginners. Powerslide/Matter; Fernandez later Rollerblade Swings, 9 Oct 2017
  • Downhill: Etienne Herreros (world no. 3 time trial 2017) — learn to slide on flat ground at low speed first (2017); Noel Jarillo — progress on small slopes; Gabe Holm (132.19 km/h world record) — full protection, slide gloves (14 Jun 2020); Alexandre Lebrun (president of the International Inline Downhill Association) — high hard-shell boot, never a low speed boot, 5-wheel frame, 83–85 mm at 85A, used gear is fine to start (15 Feb 2013).

Where skaters disagree

  • Three big wheels for a beginner. A shop channel (Thisissoul) says never above 90 mm and no three big wheels; Casper says three wheels is not necessarily bad; Joey Mantia thinks anybody can ride 3 × 125; Viktor Thorup and Bill Begg start adults on four wheels.
  • Hard or soft boot. Asha says soft boots make beginners pronate; Jan Welch and Casper accept them for comfort; Ricardo Lino says some soft boots support more than hard ones. In quad, Chicago JB skaters prefer a softer boot (Dunbeezy) while artistic champions skate stiff ones.
  • Semi-speed skates. Viktor Thorup advised against them in 2020 (video) and accepted them in 2023 as a transition or for late starters.
  • Flat or anti-rocker in aggressive: anti-rocker for Babcock, Burston, Werbeski, Orfield, Spassov; flat for Przybylik, Coburn, Salaman, Crofts, Lino. Spassov and Lino both say: try both.
  • Leather or carbon derby boots, and heel or no heel (park versus derby) — partly a matter of discipline.
  • How much protection in downhill: Daniel Ladurner says the better you get, the less you wear (vienna.at, 4 Nov 2009); Herreros, Holm, Jarillo and Lebrun wear the full set. For a beginner, the four agree.

How we compiled this, and what is missing

  • Who counts: titled skaters, team pros, recognised figures of each discipline, and coaches (a coach was a skater first). For each, we note what their reputation rests on. Where a reputation could not be verified, the page says so.
  • What we record: first the technical features they recommend or use, without brands — boot, plate or frame, wheel size and hardness, bearings, brake, fit, pads. A brand appears only when its specific feature is what the skater recommends.
  • Sponsors: noted for each skater. Many pros list their sponsor's gear; we flag it.
  • Sources: YouTube transcripts (the transcript is what counts), brand team pages, interviews, articles. Every statement has a link and a date. Paraphrase, with at most one short quote per source. Nothing is invented; a gap stays a gap.
  • Updates: the compilation will be re-run with the same method regularly — new skaters, changed setups.

What is still missing, said plainly:

  • Few of the biggest names give beginner gear advice. Top skaters publish edits and podcasts, rarely buying advice. Bart Swings, Felix Rijhnen, Sandrine Tas, Bonnie Thunders and the artistic world champions say nothing on beginner gear in what we found.
  • No artistic or dance world champion was heard describing their gear; their plates and wheels are not published.
  • Several beginner sources are coaches, shop staff or brand presenters (Casper, Kelly, Dirty Deborah Harry, Dunbeezy) rather than titled competitors.
  • The inline data leans on Powerslide team pages; the park quad data leans on Moxi videos; the artistic data is entirely Edea athletes.
  • Derby gear profiles date from 2010–2016. No titled derby player gives numeric beginner advice.
  • Downhill champions give no wheel size or hardness; no speed skater talks about brakes.
  • Instagram, TikTok and podcasts with transcripts were barely explored.

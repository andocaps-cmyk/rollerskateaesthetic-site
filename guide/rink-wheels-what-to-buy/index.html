Hard indoors, soft outdoors

  • Dunbeezy (Dunbeezytv, a YouTube teacher of US regional rhythm styles with about 24,500 subscribers; skating since his teens; reputation outside YouTube not verified; no sponsor stated, product links in the description) sums up the rule as “soft wheels outdoors, hard wheels indoors”: hard wheels absorb bumps badly, so they belong on a smooth floor. Exception: a very smooth outdoor floor, like a dance pavilion or tennis court, takes indoor wheels (Dunbeezytv, 7 Jan 2025).
  • Kim Manning (presented as a professional roller skater by the Dance Skate channel; record not verified; no sponsor stated) keeps two sets: hard indoor wheels, one of them 96A, and a separate softer pair for outside. This comes from the video description, not from her speech: the video has no transcript (Dance Skate Ep. 6, 11 May 2020).
  • Katie (@ladybird_dirtt, an Instagram rink skater of limited, unverified reach) rides 57 mm 101A wheels at the rink (Dog Days Magazine, 18 Jul 2021, updated 14 Oct 2024).

Hardness by style: 99-101A for rhythm, 103A+ to slide, 92-98A for Atlanta

  • Rhythm (New York, snapping, JB): Dunbeezy rides about 57 x 30 mm at about 101A; before that he was on 99A-100A, which he calls the more careful choice for a less advanced skater (Dunbeezytv, 7 Jan 2025).
  • Detroit (sliding): 103A and up, with the material chosen for the slide: clay grips more, wood slides more, fibreglass slides most. California style uses smaller ground-down wheels: smaller wheel, longer slide (Dunbeezytv, 7 Jan 2025). In his skate-buying video he names Fomac hard clay wheels for Detroit (Dunbeezytv, 12 Aug 2024).
  • Atlanta: speed-type wheels, about 62 x 42 mm at 92A-98A, wider and softer to hold the turns (Dunbeezytv, 7 Jan 2025).
  • Disagreement on diameter: small rink wheels for pivots and slides (Dunbeezy, about 57 mm) against 62-65 mm for an adult beginner from Kelly, an instructor at Skate Base London (local reputation; the school sells lessons, not wheels) (Skate Base London, 9 Sep 2024). Kelly is choosing for stability on a first pair; Dunbeezy is choosing for rhythm footwork. No one in our compilation settles it with numbers for a first rink session.

Derby indoors: 88A to 98A, picked by the floor

  • Scald Eagle (Rose City Rollers and Team USA jammer, WFTDA champion 2015 and 2016; tied to Bont, sponsorship not formally verified): choose a wheel on hardness, not colour; 88A-92A on polished concrete, 95A-98A on a sticky wooden floor; jammers favour control and stopping over speed, blockers softer wheels (Bont blog, 14 Sep 2020; brand text, exact attribution not verified; setup on WFTDA, 1 Oct 2015).
  • Jackie Daniels (Windy City Rollers, skater in the film Whip It, coach) rode 93A and 95A wheels in 2010 (WFTDA, 1 Mar 2010).
  • Suzy Hotrod (Gotham Girls jammer since 2004, WFTDA champion 2008, Team USA) rode narrow wheels and changed hardness with the floor (WFTDA, 2 Nov 2011).
  • Bonnie Thunders (Gotham and Rose City, several WFTDA titles and World Cups) has a signature RollerBones wheel, 62 x 38 mm in 94A or 96A with an aluminium hub — a commercial tie; her 2010 setup used derby wheels on an aluminium plate (WFTDA, Aug 2010).
  • Kitty Decapitate (London Rollergirls blocker) mixes hardnesses foot by foot on 62 x 35 mm wheels, the hardest (96A) on the inside back left for her one-foot plough (Double Threat Skates, undated; a skate shop page, commercial tie).
  • Note: these derby setups date from 2010-2015 and may not be what the players ride today.

From the skater behind this site

  • 78A-85A outside, 95A and up on the rink. A rink floor is already smooth, so a hard wheel rolls faster on it; tarmac has grain, and a hard wheel sends all of it into your feet. That is why the same skates feel like different skates after a wheel change. The hardness is printed on the wheel: a number followed by A, higher is harder.
  • Quad caveat: the skater behind this site learned on quads but has skated inline for most of his life and does not skate quads now; the style-by-style numbers above are the rink skaters’.

What rink wheels should a beginner buy?

Beginner FAQ

Can I use outdoor wheels at the rink?

You can, but they will feel slow and grippy on a smooth floor. Dunbeezy's rule is soft wheels outdoors, hard wheels indoors, and Kim Manning keeps a separate softer pair for outside and hard wheels (one set 96A) for the rink. The skater behind this site puts the rink line at 95A and up. See outdoor vs indoor wheels.

What hardness should a beginner buy for the rink?

Around 95A to 100A rather than the hardest wheels. Dunbeezy skated 99A-100A before moving to about 101A for rhythm and calls the softer step the careful choice for a less advanced skater; Katie rides 101A. Very hard 103A and up is for Detroit-style sliding.

What size rink wheel should I get?

It depends on the style. Dunbeezy rides about 57 x 30 mm for New York, snapping and JB rhythm, and wider 62 x 42 mm speed-type wheels for Atlanta. Kelly, an instructor at Skate Base London, tells adult beginners 62-65 mm. Small wheels pivot and slide; bigger ones roll and carry speed.

Are derby wheels different from rink wheels?

Usually softer and wider for grip. The derby players we compiled ride 88A to 98A indoors, picked by floor: 88-92A on polished concrete, 95-98A on a sticky wooden floor (Scald Eagle via Bont), 93-95A for Jackie Daniels, 94-96A for Bonnie Thunders' signature wheel.

Do I need wheels with a hub?

No skater in our compilation gives a beginner a rule on hubs. Bonnie Thunders' signature wheel and the RollerBones Turbo have an aluminium hub; Dunbeezy's slide wheels for Detroit are judged on their material (clay grips more, wood and fibreglass slide more). That is a gap, not a rule.

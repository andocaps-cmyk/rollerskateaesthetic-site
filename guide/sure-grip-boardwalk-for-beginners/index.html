Who it suits, and who should look elsewhere

It suits a beginner who wants a leather-boot quad that is ready to skate out of the box, light on the foot, usable outdoors and indoors, and who is happy to upgrade the plate later rather than buy a heavier metal-plate skate now.

Look elsewhere if you already know you will jump, press deep edges or skate hard: the nylon plate is the part Nicole Fiore did not trust for that, and a metal-plate skate (Lisa J, Dunbeezy) is the safer buy from the start. If you have very wide feet, read our wide-feet guide first. If you have not chosen between quad and inline yet, start with quad vs inline: which to buy first.

Questions

Is the Sure-Grip Boardwalk good for beginners?

On paper it meets four of the five checks the quad coaches in our compilation give: a leather boot (Dunbeezy, Lisa J), 82A outdoor wheels (Lisa J, Dirty Deborah Harry), 62-65 mm wheels (Kelly) and an adjustable toe stop (Dirty Deborah Harry). It misses the metal plate (Lisa J, Dunbeezy): the plate is nylon. Nicole Fiore, a coach and four-time world champion by her own account, calls it a really great purchase for a beginner after a few months on one; no skater in our compilation recommends it by name.

Is the Sure-Grip Boardwalk good for outdoor skating?

It is sold as an outdoor skate, with 82A wheels of 62 mm (65 mm on the black and beige versions). That is inside the soft, larger-wheel range Lisa J, Dirty Deborah Harry and Kelly advise for outdoors. Nicole Fiore skated hers both outside and on a rink floor.

Should I upgrade the plate on a Sure-Grip Boardwalk?

Not to start. Nicole Fiore skated beginner skills, spins and one-foot tricks on the nylon plate and found it light and easy; she advises a metal plate once you jump hard or press deep edges, which matches what Lisa J and Dunbeezy ask of a first skate. See aluminium vs nylon plates.

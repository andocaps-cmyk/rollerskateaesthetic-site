Who it suits, and who should look elsewhere

It suits someone who wants the classic rink look at the lowest price the brand name allows, skates smooth floors or paths now and then, and accepts that the wheels and toe stop are the first things they will want to replace.

Look elsewhere if you intend to learn properly, skate outdoors on rough ground, or try a skatepark. Both skaters who tried the entry-level Chicago say the wheels do not grip and that everything feels harder and scarier; Indy Jamma Jones points beginners one step up, to a Candi GRL or an Impala, and says that replacing the wheels and toe stop on a Chicago costs about what the step up would. Questions

Are Chicago roller skates good for beginners?

Are Chicago skates good for outdoor skating?

The listing does not give a wheel hardness, so we cannot say. Both skaters who tried the entry-level Chicago describe hard, plasticky wheels that did not grip on concrete or on a rink floor; Lindsey Skates says a smooth trail is the safest use. Lisa J and Dirty Deborah Harry advise soft wheels outdoors.

What is the difference between the leather-lined Chicago and the cheaper Chicago?

Chicago sells more than one quad under its name. The listing on this page is the leather-lined one, with a leather upper, an aluminium plate and urethane wheels on its spec sheet.

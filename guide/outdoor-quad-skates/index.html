Who we are quoting

  • Carolina “Caro” Hernandez: Colombian bowl skater of the Concrete Is Beautiful generation, former derby player; rides for Moxi, and the video below is Moxi’s.
  • Dunbeezy: teaches rhythm skating on YouTube (Dunbeezytv, about 24,500 subscribers); his standing outside YouTube is not verified; product links in his descriptions, no declared sponsor.
  • Dirty Deborah Harry: coach behind Dirty School of Skate (about 299,000 subscribers); her competition record is not verified; the video was shot at a skate shop, and she says she backs no brand.
  • Lisa J (Lisa Jaspers): outdoor skating creator and coach who says she has 35 years of skating, not independently verified; her own site probably carries affiliate links.
  • Estro Jen (Michelle Steilen): founder of Moxi Skates and a leading figure of quad park skating; brand owner.

Wheels: soft outside, hard inside

  • Dunbeezy’s base rule is soft wheels outdoors and hard wheels indoors, because hard wheels absorb shocks poorly. His exception: a very smooth outdoor floor, like a dance pavilion or a tennis court, takes indoor wheels (Dunbeezytv, 7 Jan 2025).
  • Caro rides her street skate with its stock soft “gummy” wheels. Soft wheels put less impact on the knees — she used them after knee surgery — and on unpolished ground, “if you are a beginner this is amazing” (Moxi, 7 Oct 2020).
  • Dirty Deborah Harry: wheels are the part you change — soft outdoors, hard indoors, hard at the skatepark (Dirty School of Skate, 21 Jun 2022). Lisa J also wants soft, gummy wheels outside (Boardwalk Skater, 25 Apr 2023, updated 26 Sep 2023).
  • Estro Jen tells park beginners not to bring their soft outdoor wheels to the skatepark — outdoor wheels are, for her too, the soft ones (Estro Jen, 9 Jan 2021). Her outdoor tips video talks technique, not gear (Moxi, 3 May 2020).
  • No skater gives a number. Moxi’s wheel guide, unsigned and undated, puts outdoor at 78A-90A with a large diameter (Moxi, read 7 Oct 2026) — a brand, not a skater.
  • Size. Dunbeezy: bigger wheels for distance in town (they roll over obstacles and go faster); for dance outside, a wheel close to the indoor size but a little taller, around 62 x 30 mm, so the plate does not rub; for distance, a wider, softer jam wheel (Dunbeezytv, 7 Jan 2025).
  • No “indoor/outdoor” wheel. Dunbeezy sees generic wheels sold for both as a warning sign on a cheap skate: one wheel cannot do both (Dunbeezytv, 13 Aug 2024).

Boot: firm, padded, real leather or suede

  • Lisa J calls the boot the most important part: firm and padded, leather or suede (Boardwalk Skater, 2023).
  • Dunbeezy: real leather, because synthetic does not shape to the foot; a bright-coloured boot often signals a non-leather one (Dunbeezytv, 13 Aug 2024).
  • Dirty Deborah Harry likes a boot with no heel or a minimal heel for derby and also outside; the boot should fit “like a slipper”, and ankle support exists in entry-level skates too (Dirty School of Skate, 2022).

Plate and cushions

  • Metal, never plastic: Lisa J (aluminium or magnesium) and Dunbeezy (aluminium; a jump bar adds stability).
  • On the street, Caro runs softer cushions than at the park, for more mobility (Moxi, 7 Oct 2020). One detailed opinion only.

Toe stop

  • Adjustable: Dunbeezy counts it among his three quality markers; Dirty Deborah Harry calls a fixed “bullseye” stop entry level.
  • Caro sets her street toe stop lower, to push and stop fast, and judges a wheel by how well she can stop on it (Moxi, 7 Oct 2020).

Disagreements and gaps

  • Heel height: Dirty Deborah Harry prefers little or no heel outside; Caro rides a stock high-top Moxi street skate. Nobody compares the two outdoors.
  • No hardness number, bearing advice or pad advice specific to outdoor skating from a skater in our compilation. Lisa J’s and Dunbeezy’s standing is not independently verified; Caro and Estro Jen are tied to Moxi.

From the skater behind this site

  • 78A to 85A outside, 95A and up on a rink floor. Pavement has grain; a hard wheel sends all of it into your feet, a soft one gives a little and absorbs it. That is why the same skates feel like different skates after a wheel change. See why your skates feel awful outside.
  • Quad caveat: the skater behind this site learned on quads but has skated inline for most of his life and does not skate quads now; take his quad points with care. His outdoor quad pages draw on a certified coach: outside is harder, not impossible, the fall to watch is backwards, a hip bounce keeps wheels out of gaps, a turn is also a stop, outdoor turns are whole-body moves.

Beginner FAQ

What wheel hardness do I need for outdoor quad skates?

Soft. Caro Hernandez, Dunbeezy, Dirty Deborah Harry and Lisa J all say soft wheels outside, but none of them gives a number in our sources. The numbers come from the skater behind this site (78A to 85A outdoors) and from Moxi's unsigned wheel guide (78A to 90A).

Can I use the same quad skates indoors and outdoors?

Yes, by swapping wheels. Dirty Deborah Harry says any pair will do at first and you change wheels for the place: soft outdoors, hard indoors. Dunbeezy adds that a very smooth outdoor floor, like a tennis court, takes indoor wheels.

Are indoor/outdoor wheels a good compromise?

Dunbeezy says no: he treats generic wheels sold as both indoor and outdoor as a warning sign on a cheap skate, because one wheel cannot do both jobs.

What should I check on a cheap outdoor quad skate?

Dunbeezy names three things: a real leather boot, an aluminium plate and an adjustable toe stop. Lisa J also rules out plastic plates, and Dirty Deborah Harry calls a fixed bullseye toe stop entry level.

Are soft outdoor wheels easier for a beginner?

Caro Hernandez says so: soft wheels put less impact on the knees and suit beginners on rough, unpolished ground. Dunbeezy gives the reason: hard wheels absorb shocks poorly.

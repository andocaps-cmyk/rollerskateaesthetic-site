Hard Boot or Soft Boot Skates? What Top Skaters Say

Written 7 October 2026 · updated 23 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Most skaters we compiled tell an adult beginner to start in a hard boot, for ankle support: Asha, Alex Shulgan and Jan Welch among them, and Asha says beginners in soft boots pronate more. Casper (Powerslide) accepts a soft boot if your ankles stay straight. Ricardo Lino says the shell and the cuff height matter more than the label.

Of the 17 skaters, coaches and gear specialists we compiled on this question, the larger group tells an adult beginner to start in a hard (rigid) boot for ankle support: Asha, Alex Shulgan, Jan Welch and the Thisissoul shop on inline. Casper (Powerslide) accepts a soft boot if your ankles stay straight, and Ricardo Lino says the label matters less than the shell and the cuff height. On quads and in speed skating the split runs differently, and both camps are named below.

Should a beginner choose a hard or soft boot?

Features first, brands only where a skater recommends a brand for a specific feature. Where a skater works for or owns the brand they talk about, we say so.

Inline: the hard-boot camp

  • Asha, coach at Skatefresh, the London skate school, says anyone heavier than a child or a small woman needs a rigid boot; beginners in soft boots are, in her words, “twice as likely to pronate” (roll onto the inside edge). No sponsor declared. (Skatefresh, 2 Feb 2021) In 2018 she called her rigid Powerslide Hardcore Evo the most comfortable boot she had worn; she skates Powerslide without a declared deal. (SkatefreshAsha, 21 Jun 2018)
  • Alex Shulgan, professional skater and co-owner of In Move Skates (commercial interest), tells beginners to start in a rigid boot. (NBC News, 9 Sep 2024)
  • Jan Welch, editorial director of Big Wheel Blading, 30 years in the industry (owner of the wheel brand Dream Urethane), says a rigid boot is more stable for a first skate. (Big Wheel Blading, updated 14 Apr 2026)
  • The Thisissoul shop in Amsterdam (a retailer, presenter not named) wants a skate as rigid as possible sideways while still flexing forward, and says a rigid boot with a removable liner is the best value around 200-250 euros. (Thisissoul, 20 Sep 2020)

Inline: the soft-boot camp

  • Casper, presenter of Powerslide’s official channel (Powerslide staff, four years in a skate shop, says he ran a marathon in soft boots), says a hard boot is the answer if your ankles collapse inward; otherwise a soft boot is more comfortable, easier to put on and acceptable. (Powerslide, 11 Mar 2024)
  • Ricardo Lino, a pro since the late 1990s with one of the largest skating channels (sells his own clothing brand), says hard or soft does not tell the whole story: support comes from the boot’s internal shell, the cuff height (a higher cuff supports more) and the materials, while comfort comes mostly from the inner foam. He sees soft boots as good for distance and ventilation, hard boots for tricks and impacts, more durable, with a washable liner. (Ricardo Lino, 2 Aug 2017)
  • The disagreement, in their own terms: Asha advises against soft boots for almost every adult; Welch and Casper call them acceptable; Lino says some soft boots support more than some hard ones. None of the soft-boot voices is a titled competitor, and Casper speaks for a brand that sells both.

Aggressive: a stiff boot, then made to flex

  • Chris Haffey, Remz pro (sponsor Remz), cuts a V into the cuff, runs the strap outside and removes the buckle so the boot gets slimmer, and invites skaters to experiment with the boot rather than keep it stock. (Remz, 8 Oct 2008) No aggressive pro in our compilation gives this as beginner advice. See first aggressive skates.

Quad: rigid artistic boot against the soft Chicago JB boot

  • The artistic and dance champions all skate a high, rigid artistic boot: Luca Lucaroni (world champion 2016-2019, Edea page), Rebecca Tarlazzi (world champion 2019, Edea page), Pau Garcia Domeç (world champion 2021, Edea page), Natalia Baldizzone Morales (solo dance world champion 2021, Edea page), Llorenç Alvarez Caballero (European silver 2013, Edea page). All are sponsored by the same brand, Edea; the pages are undated and were read on 7 October 2026.
  • Park riders want firm too. Carolina “Caro” Hernandez (Moxi rider, ex-derby) skates a rigid heeled boot on ramps and says she cannot stand soft boots there (Moxi, 7 Oct 2020). Kid Ace (Moxi team) rides the stiffer version of her boot and calls a supportive boot essential for jumping and landing hard (Moxi, 25 Sep 2020).
  • The other camp: Chicago JB. Dunbeezy, who teaches rhythm skating on YouTube (reputation outside YouTube not verified), says Chicago JB skaters use a high boot that is softer than average, and that the softness forces you to work on control. His beginner test for a cheap quality quad: leather boot, aluminium plate, adjustable stop. (Dunbeezytv, 12 Aug 2024)
  • Dirty Deborah Harry, coach behind Dirty School of Skate (about 299,000 subscribers; video shot at Wicked Skatewear), says the boot should fit “like a slipper” without relying on size for support, and that ankle support exists on entry-level skates too. (Dirty School of Skate, 21 Jun 2022)

Speed: Viktor Thorup and the semi-speed boot

  • Viktor Hald Thorup, two-time Olympic ice speed skater, Rollerblade World Team, owner of a skate shop (conflict of interest), tells an adult beginner to start on a fitness skate with a high cuff and a soft shell, and to move to a low speed boot only when ready; he says he went low too young and his ankle sagged. A high cuff gives more support for weak ankles or little experience, at the cost of mobility. (Viktor Thorup, 25 Jul 2023; Viktor Thorup, 6 Feb 2021)
  • He changed his mind on semi-speed (a high carbon cuff on a speed base). In 2020 he advised never buying them: heavy, they limit ankle flex, cause blisters and bad habits (Viktor Thorup, 28 Dec 2020). In 2023 he kept his preference but accepted them as a transition, and as a lasting choice for people who start late or skate ultra distances; he co-developed one.

What the skater behind this site adds

  • A rigid boot hurts a bit, even a good one. Pain from a rigid shoe is part of skating, not a defect; bad-quality skates are what really hurt, new or not. See why skates hurt.
  • The question is mostly an inline one: inline boots are rigid shells by construction, while quads are very often built soft, like sneakers, so they usually hurt less early on.
  • Size by the brand, tighten the fastenings: read the brand’s chart, not your street size, and follow its advice; “tight” means laces, straps and buckle, not a smaller skate. See how to size roller skates and never loosen your laces.
  • Wheels do not change the boot question: 78A-85A outside, 95A and up on a rink floor.

What do most skaters agree on?

  • Support at the ankle comes first for a beginner: Asha, Shulgan, Welch, Thisissoul, Thorup, and Casper as soon as the ankles fold in.
  • Look at the shell and the cuff height, not just the word “soft” or “hard” (Lino, Thorup on cuff height).
  • Fit decides comfort: heel locked (Casper), sized by the brand’s chart (the skater behind this site), slipper-like on quads (Dirty Deborah Harry).
  • Soft is a choice for later or for a style: distance (Lino, Casper), Chicago JB (Dunbeezy), semi-speed for late starters (Thorup 2023).

FAQ

Are hard boot or soft boot inline skates better for beginners?

The skaters disagree. Asha (Skatefresh), Alex Shulgan and Jan Welch tell adult beginners to start in a rigid boot; Asha says beginners in soft boots pronate more. Casper (Powerslide) says a soft boot is acceptable and more comfortable unless your ankles collapse inward. Ricardo Lino says the hard or soft label matters less than the shell and the cuff height.

Do soft boot skates give enough ankle support?

Some do. Ricardo Lino says support comes from the boot's internal shell, the height of the cuff and the materials, and that the hard or soft label alone does not tell you. Casper's test: if your ankles fold inward, choose a hard boot. Dirty Deborah Harry adds that ankle support exists on entry-level quads too.

Why do hard boot skates hurt at first?

According to the skater behind this site, a rigid boot produces some pain even when the skate is good; bad-quality skates hurt more, and pain that stays in one spot is usually a size or model problem. Casper says localised pain means change the model, not the size. See why skates hurt.

Should quad skates have a stiff boot?

It depends on the style. The world artistic and dance champions sponsored by Edea all skate high, rigid artistic boots; park riders Caro Hernandez and Kid Ace want a firm boot for ramps and landings. Dunbeezy says Chicago JB rhythm skaters prefer a softer boot, which forces you to work on control.

What about semi-speed skates with a high carbon cuff?

Viktor Thorup, two-time Olympic speed skater, warned against them in 2020 (heavy, limit ankle flex, blisters) and, in 2023, accepted them as a transition or for people who start late or skate ultra distances. He suggests adult beginners start in a high-cuff fitness skate.

Should I size up for a soft boot?

No: follow the brand's own size chart, measured in millimetres, and do the fastenings up tight, says the skater behind this site. Casper had to go one size down and says your heel should be locked: "think ski boot". Use the size calculator.

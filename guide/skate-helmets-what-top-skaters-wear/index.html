Skate Helmets: What Top Skaters Wear and Recommend

Written 7 October 2026 · updated 29 October 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Wear a helmet from your first session: every skater and coach in our compilation who advises beginners says so. Choose a hard-shell skate helmet that fits your head; S1, Triple Eight and Pro-Tec are the ones in the derby and park pros' gear lists. Downhill racers wear full-face helmets. Nobody here lab-tests helmets.

Of the 20 named skaters and coaches in our compilation who talk about helmets or list one in their setup — derby champions, park pros, an aggressive pro, downhill racers and inline coaches — every one who gives a beginner advice says to wear a helmet from the first session, and park skater Estro Jen puts it first. What they wear is a hard-shell skate helmet (S1, Triple Eight, Pro-Tec in the derby and park gear lists) or, in downhill, a full-face helmet; Alex Shulgan prefers a skate helmet to a bike helmet because skaters fall backwards, and Viktor Thorup says to choose on fit and try it on. Nobody in the compilation tests helmets or compares their protection: this page reports what they wear and say, nothing more.

Which helmet do skaters and coaches recommend?

Features first, brands only where a skater wears or names one. Where a skater rides for the brand they wear, or the source is the brand's own blog, we say so.

Wear one from the first session

  • Estro Jen (Michelle Steilen, founder of Moxi Skates and a leading figure of quad park skating; the video is on her own channel, and Moxi is her brand) tells beginners at the skatepark to wear all their pads and ranks the helmet first, then wrist guards, knee pads and elbow pads (Estro Jen, 9 Jan 2021; again in Moxi, 29 Apr 2026).
  • Bobi Spassov, aggressive pro with signature Roces skates (sponsor: Roces; competition record not verified here), wore a helmet, wrist guards, elbow pads, knee pads and shin guards for his first six years; in the same video coach Ricardo Lino calls the helmet the piece that can save your life (Ricardo Lino, 12 May 2024).
  • Dustin Werbeski, street, big-wheel and off-road rider (sponsors Xsjado, Powerslide, Undercover), tells off-road beginners to wear a helmet (Big Wheel Blading, 27 Jul 2017, updated 8 Jun 2023).
  • Klaudia (presents herself as a freestyle slalom world champion; the link to Klaudia Hartmanis is not verified) asks for full protection at the start, helmet included (Klaudia & Michał, 3 Mar 2025).
  • Coaches of older beginners say the same: Liz Miller, IISA-certified inline instructor and author of three skating books, wants a helmet worn every time (Get Rolling FAQ, undated), and speed coach Bill Begg lists a helmet with knee pads and wrist guards for older skaters (Inline Planet, 24 Jul 2013). More in protective gear that actually fits.

A hard-shell skate helmet, not a bike helmet (with one dissent)

  • Alex Shulgan, professional freestyle skater and co-owner of In Move Skates (commercial interest; no title verified), calls a helmet essential and prefers a skate helmet to a bike helmet, because skaters fall backwards (NBC News Select, 9 Sep 2024).
  • Slam, founder of Chuffed Skates (brand voice, no verified competition record; she recommends S1), asks for a certified hard-shell helmet with adjustable foam pads (Chuffed, 18 Aug 2022).
  • Dissent, for children: the London skate school Skatefresh (an ICP-registered school; the FAQ names no author) makes a helmet compulsory in its lessons but accepts a bike helmet (Skatefresh FAQ, updated 4 Oct 2026). Shulgan and Skatefresh disagree; we report both.
  • What "certified" means on our product list: Triple Eight states its Certified Sweatsaver meets the US CPSC standard and ASTM F1492, the skateboarding helmet standard (Triple Eight product page, read 7 Oct 2026). That is the maker's statement; we did not check certifications ourselves, and none of the skaters names a standard. A UK junior derby pack asks for helmets meeting the WFTDA/MRDA rules (Exeter Roller Derby, undated club document), a club source, not a named skater.

What the derby and park pros wear

  • Derby (WFTDA “Featured Skater” gear lists, 2010-2016 — old setups, sponsorship often unstated): Bonnie Thunders, often called the greatest player of modern derby (8 WFTDA titles), a Triple Eight helmet (WFTDA, Aug 2010); Scald Eagle, WFTDA champion 2015 and 2016 and Team USA, an S1 (WFTDA, Oct 2015); Snot Rocket Science, Team USA 2011 and 2014, an S1 Lifer (WFTDA, 4 Oct 2016); Satan's Little Helper, three straight championships with the Arizona Derby Dames, an S-One (WFTDA, 10 Jun 2016); Nina Erwes, Helsinki captain, an S1 (WFTDA, 8 Sep 2016); Jackie Daniels, Windy City Rollers and coach, a Pro-Tec Ace (WFTDA, 1 Mar 2010).
  • Park: Caro Hernandez, Colombian bowl skater, and Tia Pitman, winner of Concrete Waves 2023 and S1-sponsored, both wear the S1 Lifer — both lists are on S1's own blog (S1, 6 Dec 2021; S1, 3 Nov 2023).
  • Source bias: S1 comes up most, but several of these lists come from S1's blog or from riders S1 sponsors. That is a count of gear lists, not a verdict on which helmet protects best.

Fit first

  • Viktor Thorup, two-time Olympic speed skater on ice and Rollerblade World Team member (he also owns a skate shop), wears a light, close-fitting helmet and tells beginners that fit comes first: try the helmet on in a shop (Viktor Thorup, 25 Jul 2023; 21 Apr 2021).
  • A Swiss teaching document for children gives a fitting cue: the helmet sits two fingers above the bridge of the nose, straps tight (mobilesport.ch, Jan-Feb 2021; an institutional source, not a titled skater). Bonnie Thunders recorded a helmet-fitting video for WFTDA (WFTDA, 12 Jul 2020), but we could not read its transcript, so we report nothing from it.
  • Size by head circumference on the maker's chart; Triple Eight says to measure starting in the middle of your forehead, and to size up when in between (Triple Eight fit guide). The protective gear checklist has the charts.

Downhill: full-face

  • Noel Jarillo, Spanish downhill skater (sponsor Flying Eagle), wears a full-face helmet with slide gloves (Big Wheel Blading, undated); Daniel Ladurner, five-time downhill world champion, lists a full-face helmet as compulsory in downhill (vienna.at, 4 Nov 2009, read in machine translation); Etienne Herreros, world no. 3 in downhill time trial in 2017 (sponsors EOskate, RollerN'co), races with a helmet plus a full set of pads and a back protector (Big Wheel Blading, 30 Aug 2017).
  • Ladurner adds that the best wear less padding because it slows them: an elite racer's remark, not advice for a beginner.

What we could not find

  • No skater in our compilation compares helmet models, gives a replacement rule after a crash, or names a safety standard. Thunders' fitting video is unread. Most derby gear lists date from 2010-2016.

From the skater behind this site

  • A helmet: you wear it a hundred times for nothing, and once for everything.
  • No ranking at the start: as a beginner, leave none of the four pieces out — helmet, wrist guards, elbow pads, knee pads. Estro Jen ranks them; he does not, and says all of them matter at first. The fall that hurts is backwards, onto the tailbone or the back of the head (see how to fall safely).

What people who skated them report

These are the reports we could find and read from people who say they actually used them, with their limits. We did not use Amazon customer reviews, which Amazon does not allow sites to reproduce.

  • Triple Eight Certified Sweatsaver Helmet: Neil Hester, a skateboarder (not a roller skater) who reviews his own gear on YouTube, had worn one for at least six months and bought a second (video from about 2024). He found the fit very snug once he picked the right size from his head circumference, with a spare set of thicker pads in the box to adjust it, and calls it the most comfortable helmet he has tried. The catch is the “sweat saver” brow pad: for a skater who sweats a lot it soaks up sweat and then drips into the eyes, so he wrings it out during sessions; the side pads go in the washing machine, but he advises against machine-washing the centre pad, whose fabric came unstuck. After several falls where his head hit the ground, the shell showed no damage. (source, watched 24 Oct 2026)

Which skate helmet should you buy?

A hard-shell skate helmet that fits your head, worn from the first session with wrist guards, elbow pads and knee pads. The Triple Eight Sweatsaver is the cheaper route with a stated certification; the S1 Lifer is the one the park and derby pros in our compilation wear most. Going downhill fast is a different case: the racers wear full-face helmets.

Beginner FAQ

Do I really need a helmet as a beginner skater?

Every skater and coach in our compilation who gives beginners advice says yes, from the first session. Estro Jen puts it first among the pads, Bobi Spassov wore one for his first six years, and coach Ricardo Lino calls it the piece that can save your life.

Is a bike helmet fine for roller skating?

The sources disagree. Alex Shulgan prefers a skate helmet because skaters fall backwards, and Slam of Chuffed Skates asks for a certified hard shell; the Skatefresh school accepts a bike helmet for children's lessons.

Which helmet do pro roller skaters wear?

In the derby and park gear lists we compiled, S1 comes up most (Scald Eagle, Snot Rocket Science, Nina Erwes, Satan's Little Helper, Caro Hernandez, Tia Pitman), then Triple Eight (Bonnie Thunders) and Pro-Tec (Jackie Daniels). Several S1 lists come from S1's own blog or sponsored riders.

What certification should a skate helmet have?

None of the skaters names a standard. Triple Eight states its Certified Sweatsaver meets the US CPSC standard and ASTM F1492; a UK junior derby club asks for WFTDA/MRDA-compliant helmets. We have not checked any certification ourselves.

How should a skate helmet fit?

Viktor Thorup says fit comes first and to try it on in a shop. Measure your head circumference starting mid-forehead and follow the maker's chart, sizing up when in between (Triple Eight). A Swiss teaching guide places the helmet two fingers above the bridge of the nose, straps tight.

Do I need a full-face helmet?

Only the downhill racers in our compilation wear one: Noel Jarillo and Daniel Ladurner, who calls it compulsory in downhill. For rink, park, derby and street, the skaters wear open hard-shell helmets.

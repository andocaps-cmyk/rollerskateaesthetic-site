What each skater says

For aluminium, and the Reactor in particular

  • Bonnie Thunders (often called the greatest player of modern derby; 8 WFTDA titles; RollerBones signature wheel) rode Powerdyne Reactor plates on Riedell 595 boots (WFTDA gear list, Aug 2010).
  • Suzy Hotrod (Gotham jammer since 2004, WFTDA champion 2008, Team USA; no sponsor stated) rode a Reactor too, and says the same gear will not make you her level, practice will (WFTDA gear list, Nov 2011).
  • Caro Hernandez (Colombian bowl skater; Moxi, Powerdyne, RollerBones, S1) rides a Reactor Pro with medium cushions and narrow trucks at the park (S1 blog, 6 Dec 2021). On the street she rides a stock Moxi Panther with softer cushions (Moxi, 7 Oct 2020).
  • Kid Ace (Moxi team rider: vert, gaps, stairs) rides a Reactor Pro with stock trucks that has never broken despite hard use, and felt more stable and faster after moving to a longer plate: choose plate length with care (Moxi, 25 Sep 2020).
  • Lisa J (Lisa Jaspers, creator and coach, 35 years of skating by her own account; her site likely carries affiliate links) tells beginners: a metal plate, aluminium or magnesium, never plastic (Boardwalk Skater, 25 Apr 2023, updated 26 Sep 2023).
  • Other derby players in our lists also ride aluminium competition plates, not the Reactor: Scald Eagle (Infinity), Swede Hurt and Snot Rocket Science (Roll-Line Killer), Juniper Simonis (Crazy Venus), Satan’s Little Helper (Boss) (WFTDA gear lists, 2015-2016).

For nylon, with conditions

  • Shove (quad skater on street and ramps, ex-derby, former Moxi team, over 350 lb by her own account) disputes the idea that nylon must break under a heavy skater: in her experience, how hard you skate (jumps, aggressive) matters more than weight — “it’s not about how much weight the plate can hold”. She still keeps metal for the park and says to check the maker’s weight limit, as some stop at 250 lb (Moxi, 5 Mar 2021).

Is a Reactor worth it?

For a first pair, our sources do not say you need one. The four Reactor riders are elite derby players and sponsored park riders. What they share with the beginner advice is the material: aluminium. Lisa J is the only voice in our compilation who addresses beginners on plates, and she says metal. Shove is the only voice for nylon, for everyday skating, not for park. And the Reactor Pro could not be bought on Amazon.com when we checked.

Which one for a beginner

From the sources: a stock skate with an aluminium plate if you can (Lisa J), and an aluminium plate if you plan to jump or ride the park (Shove, Kid Ace). A nylon plate is fine for everyday rink or trail skating if you stay within its weight limit (Shove). An upgrade to a Reactor-class plate comes later, if at all. The skater behind this site learned on quads but has skated inline for most of his life and does not skate quads now, so on plates he defers to the quad skaters above.

Beginner FAQ

Is an aluminium plate better than nylon for a beginner?

Lisa J, the one voice in our compilation who speaks to beginners about plates, says metal, never plastic. Shove says reinforced nylon holds up for everyday skating, even at over 350 lb, but keeps metal for the park.

Is a Powerdyne Reactor worth it?

Four skaters in our sources ride one (Bonnie Thunders, Suzy Hotrod, Caro Hernandez, Kid Ace), all at elite or sponsored level; Kid Ace says hers has never broken. None says a beginner needs one, and it was unavailable on Amazon.com on 7 October 2026.

Will a nylon plate break?

Shove's experience is that how hard you skate matters more than your weight; jumps and aggressive skating are where she uses metal. Check the maker's weight limit: some stop at 250 lb.

Which skates have an aluminium plate?

Among the listings we checked: Impala, Riedell Orbit and Bont Quadstar. The Riedell R3, Sure-Grip Rock GT50 and Moxi Lolly have nylon plates.

Does plate length matter?

Kid Ace felt more stable and faster on a longer plate and says to choose length with care. No one in our compilation gives a beginner a length in numbers.

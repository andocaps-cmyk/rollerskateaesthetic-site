What are the best wheels for outdoor roller skating?

Hardness: soft on pavement, hard on smooth floors

  • Soft outside, especially for beginners. Carolina "Caro" Hernandez, Colombian bowl skater and Moxi rider, rides her street skates on the stock soft "gummy" wheels and says they are easy on the knees and great for beginners on unpolished ground (Moxi video, 07/10/2020) — sponsor: Moxi. Lisa J (coach, 35 years on skates, self-declared) recommends soft wheels for beginners (boardwalkskater.com, 2023). Coach Dirty Deborah Harry (Dirty School of Skate) says any wheels will do at first because they swap: hard indoors, soft outdoors, hard at the skatepark (video, 21/06/2022).
  • Inline numbers that confirm 78A-85A. Jan Welch (editor of Big Wheel Blading, 30 years in the industry, owns Dream Urethane wheels) gives 80A-85A for recreational skating (bigwheelblading.com, updated 14/04/2026). Alexandre Lebrun (president of the International Inline Downhill Association) uses 85A for downhill (rollerenligne, 2013/2020). Casper, presenter of Powerslide's channel (brand video), gives 80A-88A as the usual range: softer grips and damps vibration but wears faster (video, 11/03/2024).
  • Speed skaters say the same. Viktor Hald Thorup (two-time Olympic speed skater, Rollerblade World Team, owns a skate shop) advises soft wheels for rough roads, rain and training, hard for a smooth track (video, 25/07/2023). Ewen Fernandez (multiple world champion) answered "soft" when asked his preference, without naming a surface (rollerenligne, before 2018).
  • Where hard wheels belong. Derby on indoor floors: Scald Eagle (two-time WFTDA champion) gives 88A-92A on polished concrete and 95A-98A on sticky wood (Bont blog, 14/09/2020 — Bont text, exact attribution not verified); Jackie Daniels (Windy City Rollers) rode 93A-95A (WFTDA, 2010). Dance skater Kim Manning keeps 96A wheels for indoors and a separate softer set for outdoors (listed in the video description, 11/05/2020, not spoken).

Where the 78A-85A rule is nuanced:

  • Skateparks are outside but want hard wheels. Michelle Steilen "Estro Jen" (founder of Moxi, signature RollerBones wheel) tells beginners not to bring soft gummy wheels to the park: 92A or harder from the start (video, 09/01/2021) — her own brand. Kid Ace (Moxi team) rides 101A (video, 25/09/2020); Caro goes to 103A on ramps.
  • Aggressive and slalom inline sit a little higher. Street bladers ride 88A-92A as a base (at least 11 setups in our compilation); Gabriel Talamantes (Chicago scene) runs 92A on the street and 88-90A in the park (Be-Mag, 08/12/2023), while Bryce Orfield picks the hardest he can for durability (Be-Mag, 11/07/2023). Slalom world champion Lorenzo Guslandi rides 86A (Powerslide team page) and Kim Sung Jin 85A (rollerenligne, 11/01/2011) — both sponsored.

Diameter: what a beginner should start on

  • Inline: about 80 mm, 90 mm at most — one dissenting voice. Welch: 80 mm to start, and reports an instructor calling 80 mm "perfect" for a beginner. Asha (Skatefresh coach, London) gives four 80 mm wheels or three 90-110 mm for fitness (skatefresh.com, 2021). The Thisissoul shop channel (Amsterdam, a seller) says never above 90 mm for a beginner (video, 20/09/2020). Disagreement: Thorup suggests 100 mm for adult beginners — "I suggest 100 millimeters for adult beginners" — from a speed-skating angle.
  • Big wheels roll rough ground better, small ones reassure. Casper: large wheels pass rough surfaces more easily, small wheels lower your centre of gravity.
  • Quad: 62 mm is the common beginner pick. Estro Jen would choose 62 mm over 57 mm for a beginner at the skatepark: more speed for less effort (video, 31/01/2021) — her signature wheel. Kelly, instructor at Skate Base London (local reputation), gives 62-65 mm for adult beginners (blog, 09/09/2024).

Bearings: not the upgrade

  • Thorup: ABEC 7 or 9 is enough; good bearings don't win races, bad ones are just a nuisance. Casper and Thisissoul: keep the stock bearings at first. Nobody in the compilation links a higher ABEC number to beginner speed.

Wheels are the cheapest thing to change — but the skate comes first

  • Dirty Deborah Harry: you can keep the same skates and just change wheels. Casper: choose the skate for the boot, not the wheels, and prefer a removable frame (165 mm standard or Trinity) so you can change wheel size later. Asha: technique matters more than wheel size.

What the skater behind this site adds

Stay in 78A-85A outside. That band comes from the skater behind this site. The compilation confirms it for pavement (Welch 80A-85A, Lebrun 85A, soft wheels for beginners from Caro, Lisa J, Dirty and Thorup) and nuances it in two places: skateparks want hard wheels even outdoors, and street aggressive and slalom setups ride 85A-92A.

FAQ

What hardness should outdoor skate wheels be?

Soft: roughly 78A-85A on pavement. Jan Welch gives 80A-85A for recreational inline, Casper (Powerslide) 80A-88A, and Caro Hernandez, Lisa J, Dirty Deborah Harry and Viktor Thorup all advise soft wheels for rough ground. The hardness is printed on the wheel as a number followed by A; try the wheel hardness picker and see outdoor vs indoor wheels.

Should I use soft wheels at the skatepark?

No. Estro Jen tells beginners to bring 92A or harder to the park, not soft gummy wheels; Kid Ace and Caro ride 101A-103A on ramps.

What wheel size should a beginner start on?

Inline: about 80 mm, 90 mm at most (Jan Welch, Asha, Thisissoul); Viktor Thorup disagrees and suggests 100 mm for adults. Quad: 62 mm (Estro Jen) to 65 mm (Kelly, Skate Base London). Your plate or frame sets the maximum — Sure-Grip's LoRide plate takes 37, 47 or 57 mm depending on the riser, Rollerblade's Blank frame takes 58 mm (Inline Warehouse product pages). See big wheels lift you higher.

Can I use outdoor wheels on a rink?

Yes, according to Riedell, but soft wheels "may feel sluggish/grippy on smooth floors". Skaters who ride both keep two sets: Kim Manning uses 96A indoors and softer wheels outside.

Do expensive bearings make me faster?

No. Viktor Thorup says ABEC 7 or 9 is enough; Casper and Thisissoul say to keep the stock bearings. See skate bearings explained.

Do I need new skates to skate outside?

Usually not. Dirty Deborah Harry: keep the skates and change the wheels. See when to replace wheels and, for inline, rotate your wheels — Gabriel Talamantes rotates his too.

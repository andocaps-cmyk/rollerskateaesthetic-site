What protective gear do the WFTDA rules require?

From The Rules of Flat Track Roller Derby, version 20250101 (the version shown on the rules site, read 11 October 2026):

  • Glossary, “Protective Gear”: skaters must wear a helmet, mouthguard, wrist guards, elbow pads and knee pads. The entry adds that a piece is required so long as it gives the skater added protection and the skater is physically able to wear it, and that a skater is not penalised when a piece gives no added protection.
  • Section 1.2 (Teams, in section 1, Game Parameters & Safety): skaters wear quad-style roller skates and protective gear during play; inline skates are not permitted; protective gear may not be removed during play, and may not endanger or interfere with other skaters, support staff or officials.
  • Section 4.4.4 (Protective Gear in the Penalty Box): seated in the box, a skater may take out the mouthguard and no other gear, and must put it back before leaving.
  • What the rules page does not say: we found no helmet certification standard, no mouthguard definition and no pad specification in it. Leagues and their insurers can add their own; ask yours before buying the helmet.

What gear do derby players actually wear?

From the WFTDA “Featured Skater” profiles of 2010-2016: they show what each player used at that date, not necessarily today, and a listed brand may be sponsored even when the profile does not say so.

Helmet: 6 of 11 players name one

  • Bonnie Thunders, the jammer often called the greatest player of modern derby (four World Cups, eight WFTDA titles with Gotham and Rose City), wore a Triple 8 helmet (WFTDA, 1 Aug 2010). Jackie Daniels, Windy City Rollers skater and coach who skated in Whip It, a Pro-Tec Ace (WFTDA, 1 Mar 2010).

Wrist guards and elbow pads: named by few

  • Scald Eagle listed Elle wrist guards and hockey elbow pads (Reebok); Swede Hurt listed Ennui wrist guards. No other derby profile names either piece by itself.
  • Others list a pad brand without saying which pieces: Atomatrix, Oly Rollers jammer and 17-time world inline speed gold medallist, Atom Gear (WFTDA, 8 Sep 2011); Smarty Pants, Team USA co-captain and coach since 2003, Pro Designed, her sponsor (WFTDA, May 2013); Nina Erwes, Steaks; Jackie Daniels, Triple Eight.

Mouthguard: required, and named by one player

  • Snot Rocket Science lists a Sisu mouthguard. She is the only one of the 11. The rules make it compulsory anyway, and it is the only piece you may take out in the penalty box.
  • Gap: no player in our compilation explains how to choose or fit a mouthguard. The SISU listing sizes it by height: Medium for 5’–6’, and a Small exists.

Which piece first? None of them

  • Estro Jen (Michelle Steilen, Moxi founder; a park skater, not a derby player) ranks them for beginners: helmet, then wrist guards, the most common skating injury, then knees, then elbows (Estro Jen, 9 Jan 2021).
  • The skater behind this site does not rank them: at the start every piece matters, with no hierarchy, and a beginner leaves none out. Derby has already settled the argument — the rules require all five.

From the skater behind this site: what each piece is for

  • Wrist guards take the forward fall. Putting a bare palm down at speed is a reflex faster than any decision, and it is the reflex that breaks wrists.
  • Elbow pads take the backward fall — as he puts it, they are what you catch yourself on. Falling backwards, the elbows land before anything else, and it is the fall nobody expects.
  • Knee pads change what falling means: with them you can drop forward on purpose instead of fighting to stay up, and a chosen fall stops a wobble before it becomes a real one.
  • The helmet: you wear it a hundred times for nothing and once for everything.
  • Caveat: he skated mostly inline and learned quad without continuing. This is why each pad exists, not derby-specific advice.

How should roller derby pads fit?

  • Size by the brand’s chart. The skater behind this site, on sizing: listen to the brand, it knows its own sizes better than he does. How is derby gear different from recreational pads?

  • The mouthguard: required in WFTDA play, absent from every recreational kit.
  • No choice: recreational skaters decide what to wear; in WFTDA play all five pieces are compulsory and stay on.
  • Knee pads: the players name derby lines (187 Pro Derby, DeadBolt, 187 gel). The 187 Pro Derby listing describes a removable “Lock In Cap” shell, removable washable foam and an anti-slip “Fusion Groove Channel”. A three-piece recreational set like the Triple Eight Saver Series is a start, not what the players list.
  • Elbows: Scald Eagle went to hockey elbow pads rather than skate ones.
  • Quad only: section 1.2 does not permit inline skates in play. For the skate itself, see your first roller derby skates.

Beginner FAQ

What protective gear do I need for roller derby?

The WFTDA rules (version 20250101, glossary entry Protective Gear) list five pieces: helmet, mouthguard, wrist guards, elbow pads and knee pads. Section 1.2 says skaters must wear protective gear during play and may not take it off during play. Your league’s own rules and insurance come on top.

Is a mouthguard required in roller derby?

Yes under WFTDA rules: the mouthguard is one of the five pieces in the Protective Gear glossary entry. Section 4.4.4 lets a skater take out the mouthguard, and nothing else, while seated in the penalty box, and it must go back in before leaving. Among the 11 derby players in our compilation, only Snot Rocket Science names a mouthguard (Sisu).

How is derby gear different from recreational skating pads?

Derby adds the mouthguard, makes all five pieces compulsory during play, and is quad only (section 1.2 does not permit inline skates). The players lean to dedicated knee pads: Scald Eagle listed 187 Pro Derby knee pads and hockey elbow pads, Swede Hurt DeadBolt knee pads, Bonnie Thunders 187 gel knee pads.

Which pad matters most for a derby beginner?

None can be skipped. Estro Jen, a park skater, ranks them helmet, wrist guards, knees, elbows. The skater behind this site says that at the start every piece matters and there is no hierarchy. In WFTDA derby the rules settle it: all five are required.

How should derby pads fit?

Use each brand’s own size chart. Slam, founder of Chuffed Skates, says pads should be neither too tight nor slipping. The skater behind this site says tight applies to the fastenings, not to the size: follow the brand on size, do the straps up firmly. See protective gear that actually fits.

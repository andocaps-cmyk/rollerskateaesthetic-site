Best Roller Skates for Wide Feet

Written 7 October 2026 · updated 23 November 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Across seven skaters and coaches we compiled (aggressive and urban inline, speed inline, quad derby), the advice for wide feet starts in the same place: pick the boot for the shape and width of your foot first, the brand second — Bruce, DiPaolo, Bell and Lomax chose their boots that way, and coach Dirty Deborah Harry says a quad boot should fit like a slipper. In practice that means a skate its maker or stockist sells as wide-cut, sized from a foot measured in millimetres, with a boot cut for what you will skate. For cruising outside on quads, Moxi's stockist names the Rainbow Rider and the Beach Bunny, both high-top recreational boots. For roller derby, leagues want a low-cut boot that lets the ankle bend: look at the Chaya Ruby or the Crazy VXI, and ask your league before you buy.

What top skaters recommend

Boot: chosen for foot shape and width, before the brand

This is the strongest agreement in our compilation. Dominic Bruce (2nd, Winterclash 2019 pro) says to pick the boot that suits the shape of your body to spare yourself long-term pain, and blames a lasting ankle injury on a boot that did not suit him [sponsor: Razors] (Be-Mag, 2018). Andrew DiPaolo (New York blader, 12th & D Classic) has skated Razors for 13 years because they suit his wide foot [sponsor: Razors] (Be-Mag, 2017). Hazen Bell (Razors team rider) picked a boot and liner that are comfortable for wide feet [sponsor: Razors, Reign] (Be-Mag, 2019). Nick Lomax (IMYTA champion 2006, Masters of Real Street Prague 2018) chose his urban boot because it “fits me like a glove”, per his sponsor's video description [sponsor: Powerslide] (Powerslide, 2021).

Note the sponsor pattern: three of these riders are Razors riders praising Razors' fit. That is their own brand, so read it as “fit decides”, not as “buy Razors”. These are aggressive and urban inline skaters; none of them is talking about beginner skates.

Fit: a quad boot should feel like a slipper

Dirty Deborah Harry, coach behind the Dirty School of Skate channel (about 299,000 subscribers; her derby record was not verified in our compilation), says a quad boot should fit comfortably, like a slipper, without relying on extra length for support; your foot does not get longer, and if the width pinches, she says to go up a size (Know This BEFORE Buying Roller Skates, 2022). The video was filmed at a skate shop; its description says it endorses no brand.

Sizing: length first, then width; moulding can help

Viktor Hald Thorup, a two-time Olympic speed skater on the Rollerblade World Team, says to get the length right first and the width second, and mentions heat-moulding (oven or water) for speed boots (Finding the right Skating Boots, 2021). He owns a skate shop, which is a conflict of interest to keep in mind.

Pain in one spot: change the model, not the size

Casper, presenter of Powerslide's official channel (four years in a skate shop; an industry figure, not a competition skater) says a sharp pain in one place means trying a different model, not a different size, while mild discomfort is normal while the liner shapes to the foot [Powerslide employee] (How To Choose Your First Skates, 2024).

Where they disagree: on quads, Dirty Deborah Harry says to size up when the width pinches. Inline sources in our compilation say the opposite direction for fit - a snug skate, heel locked, no size above (Casper even went a size down) - and Casper says a pressure point means a different model, not a bigger one. The likely reading is that soft quad boots and rigid inline shells behave differently, but no skater we compiled says that in so many words.

What is missing: no top skater in our compilation names a specific skate model for wide feet in a beginner's budget. The width information on the models below comes from a shop and a retailer, not from a skater.

Starting roller derby? Do not buy these high-top skates for it. Derby skates “often feature low-cut boots for ankle flexibility” (Riedell), and one shop's derby guide warns that a boot that stops the ankle bending makes derby drills “extremely difficult and potentially very unsafe” (skates.co.uk). Lafayette Roller Derby's gear list asks new skaters not to buy Moxi, Beach Bunny or Impala skates for derby. Shops list two low-cut derby starters for normal to wider feet: the Chaya Ruby (truFIT last, “medium to wide”) and the Crazy VXI (a toe box that widens with the laces). Later, Bont makes its Hybrid derby boot in wide and double wide, to order. Riedell's R3, the usual first derby skate, suits “standard to narrow feet”, according to its stockist.

FAQ

Should I size up for wide feet?

Not by default. Bruce, DiPaolo and Bell chose a boot that suits their foot shape rather than a bigger size, and Powerslide's Casper says a pressure point means a different model, not a different size. On quads, coach Dirty Deborah Harry does say to go up a size if the width pinches. The skater behind this site adds: follow the brand's chart, from a foot measured in millimetres. See the skate size calculator and how to size roller skates.

How should a quad skate feel on a wide foot?

Like a slipper: comfortable, without counting on extra length for support, according to coach Dirty Deborah Harry. Your foot does not grow into a skate.

Should I loosen the laces to make room?

No. Tight is for the laces, straps and buckle, not the size: do the skate up firmly (the skater behind this site). If one spot still hurts when laced properly, Casper of Powerslide says to try another model. See the strap over your tendon is not too tight and your top strap jams before it is tight.

Are quads or inline skates better for wide feet?

No skater in our compilation answers this directly. The skater behind this site, who does not have wide feet, leans towards quads because they use soft, shoe-like boots rather than a rigid shell; that is reasoning, not lived experience. Some aggressive inline pros with wide feet (DiPaolo, Bell) skate rigid boots that suit them.

Can a boot be moulded to a wide foot?

Olympic speed skater Viktor Hald Thorup mentions heat-moulding (oven or water) for speed boots, after getting length right first and width second. We have no source on moulding beginner quad or inline skates.

Why do my feet hurt even though the skates fit?

Poor quality, wrong size or a rigid boot, according to the skater behind this site; mild discomfort while the liner shapes to the foot is normal, per Casper of Powerslide. See why skates hurt and two different reasons your feet hurt.

What this page does not cover: heat-moulding and boot stretching, kids' skates, and custom boots.

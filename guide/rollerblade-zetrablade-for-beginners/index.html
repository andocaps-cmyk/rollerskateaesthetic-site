She identifies a soft boot with a sturdy cuff, a liner that does not come out, a plastic buckle and a one-piece riveted frame that cannot be changed; she rates the 80 mm wheel size as right for a beginner and likes the quick Velcro strap and the heel brake fitted from the start. Her own advice is a hard boot for beginners, yet she recommends the Zetrablade to people starting out, for its comfort, ease and price, and not for speed, big hills or tricks. Who it suits, and who should look elsewhere

It suits an adult who wants a supportive, stable first skate for paths and smooth streets, and who is not sure yet how far they will take inline skating.

Look elsewhere if you already know you will want bigger wheels for distance within a season: the frame will not follow you. Questions

Is the Rollerblade Zetrablade good for beginners?

It meets the wheel points the coaches in our compilation give, four 80 mm wheels at 82A (Jan Welch, Alex Shulgan, the Thisissoul shop). It misses two: Polly Morris, who skated it, describes a soft boot where Asha, Shulgan and Welch want a rigid one, and its one-piece frame cannot take other wheel sizes, which Welch and Casper (Powerslide) advise against. Polly Morris still recommends it for starting out. No pro skater we compiled recommends it by name; Jan Welch, one of the voices we compile, reviewed it favourably in an affiliate-linked review.

Why is the Zetrablade so popular?

Beginners buy it in large numbers: in October 2026 it ranked high in Amazon.com's best-seller list for inline skates. That is popularity with buyers, not an expert recommendation.

What size Zetrablade should I get?

Follow Rollerblade's own size chart. Measure both feet in the socks you will skate in and size for the longer foot, as the retailer page for the skate advises. The men's listing mentions a footboard sizer for fit adjustment.

Can I put bigger wheels on the Zetrablade later?

Not easily. The frame is a monocoque (one-piece) composite frame built for 80 mm wheels. Casper says a one-piece frame locks you to one wheel size; Welch wants a removable frame so the skate can grow with you.

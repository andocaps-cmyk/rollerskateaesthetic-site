Your First Aggressive Inline Skates: What Top Bladers Recommend

Written 7 October 2026 · updated 24 October 2026 · by Cass · written with an AI assistant

Across the 18 aggressive bladers in our compilation, a first aggressive skate is a boot chosen for the shape of your foot, a frame and soul plate built for grinding, and small hard wheels of 56-64 mm at 88-92A, set flat or anti-rocker. Only two of them give advice aimed at beginners in so many words, Bobi Spassov and Ricardo Lino: learn to skate on regular skates first, and wear full pads including shin guards. The bladers split on flat versus anti-rocker; both camps are named below.

What top skaters recommend

Features first. Almost every setup below comes from a sponsored rider describing their sponsor’s gear; the sponsor is named each time. Most setups come from Be-Mag’s “Blade Check” column, where riders explain their setup and what wears out.

Know how to skate before you buy

  • Bobi Spassov, Israeli pro with signature Roces models (sponsor: Roces), says to know how to skate before starting aggressive: these skates are not made for long distances and give blisters. (Ricardo Lino channel, 12 May 2024)
  • From the same video: what makes a skate “aggressive” is the soul plate and the frame, not the boot (Spassov and Ricardo Lino, an instructor with close to 40 years of skating by his own account; competitive record not verified).

Boot: chosen for your foot, then tuned

  • Dominic Bruce, 2nd pro at Winterclash 2019 (sponsors: Razors, Ground Control), says pick the boot that suits your body’s shape to spare yourself long-term pain; he blames a lasting ankle injury on a boot that did not fit. (Be-Mag, 2 Oct 2018)
  • Andrew DiPaolo, New York blader (Razors) has stayed on Razors for 13 years because they suit his wide foot (Be-Mag, 25 Aug 2017); Hazen Bell (Razors, Reign) picks boot and liner for comfort on wide feet (Be-Mag, 7 Jun 2019).
  • Nick Lomax (Powerslide/USD) chose a boot because it “fits me like a glove”, a quote from the video description, about an urban skate rather than an aggressive one (Powerslide, 12 Jul 2021).
  • Liners. A better liner is common: Bruce and Bell (Reign), Gabriel Talamantes, a long-standing Chicago blader (Roces), who adds an Intuition liner to firm up a soft boot (Be-Mag, 8 Dec 2023), and Tomek Przybylik, Summerclash AM champion (USD), on MyFit liners.
  • Tuning the cuff is a matter of taste. Chris Haffey, X Games gold 2009 and 2011 (Remz at the time), cuts a V into the cuff and removes the buckle for a slimmer boot (Remz, 8 Oct 2008); Jon Julio, founder of Them Skates (his own brand), designed the Them 909 to hold the top of the foot more firmly, with a cuff you can set high or low for more or less forward flex (Jump Street Podcast, 27 Aug 2019). Neither presents it as beginner advice.

Wheels: 56-64 mm, 88-92A

  • At least eleven of the compiled setups sit in that band: Haffey 57 mm/88A, Si Coburn 59 mm/90A (Rollerblade, Lade; Be-Mag, 29 Oct 2019), Stephen Babcock 60 mm/90A (Blank, Moonshine; Be-Mag, 4 Apr 2023), Talamantes 59 mm/92A, Alex Burston 57 mm/89A (Razors, BHC; Be-Mag, 3 Apr 2015), Sam Crofts 58 mm (USD, Undercover; Be-Mag, 15 Apr 2015), Bruce 62-64 mm, Niko Salaman 64 mm (Seba, Famus; Be-Mag, 10 Aug 2020), and Spassov 60 mm.
  • Disagreement on hardness. Bryce Orfield, Midwest street blader (Blank, Undercover), runs the hardest wheels he can for durability (Be-Mag, 11 Jul 2023); Talamantes goes softer (88-90A) in the park and harder in the street, and rotates his wheels.
  • Bigger wheels exist. Przybylik (USD) and Dustin Werbeski (Xsjado, Undercover; Be-Mag, 13 Mar 2015) ride 72 mm for speed. None of them says this is a starting point.

Flat or anti-rocker: the main disagreement

  • Anti-rocker (two small hard wheels in the middle for grinding): Babcock, Burston, Werbeski, Orfield and Spassov. Spassov says it makes grinding easier.
  • Flat (four equal wheels): Przybylik, Coburn (after 20 years of anti-rocker), Salaman, Crofts (“58mm flat handles business on all terrain”) and Lino. They cite easier turns, more speed and better landings.
  • Common ground: Spassov and Lino both tell beginners to try both and choose. For a beginner who wants to grind, they present anti-rocker as the easier start.

Protective gear, shin guards included

  • Spassov wore wrist guards, elbow pads, knee pads, shin guards and a helmet for his first six years. He calls shin guards “super important” for learning grinds, and wrist guards protect against backward falls that break hands. Lino puts the helmet first and still wears pads; he adds padded shorts for hips and tailbone. (Ricardo Lino channel, 12 May 2024)

Keep it simple, keep it tight

  • Fred Bukowski, Winterclash AM champion 2015 (Seba, FLAT), rides one flat all-terrain setup (Be-Mag, 27 Dec 2018). Coburn checks that his bolts are tight; Crofts wants anything that rattles gone. What wears first: wheels, laces, soul plates and buckles (Przybylik, Bukowski, Babcock).

What we could not find

  • No top pro (Haffey, Julio, Lomax, Dre Powell, Joey Lunsford, Montre Livingston, Chris Farmer, Richie Eisler) has a readable “what to buy first” video or interview. Nobody explains sizing in words: the Them size chart is an image.

From the skater behind this site

  • Size: follow the brand’s own chart and advice, including “go up a size” when the brand says so; sizes vary a lot between makers. Measure in millimetres, standing, at the end of the day.
  • Tight means the fastenings, not the size: laces, strap and buckle done up firmly.
  • Hardness: his rule for recreational wheels is 78A-85A outside and 95A and up on a rink. Aggressive wheels at 88-92A are harder than his outdoor range: expect a firmer ride on rough ground.

Beginner FAQ

Should a beginner start on aggressive skates?

Bobi Spassov (Roces pro) says learn to skate first: aggressive skates are not made for long distances and give blisters. Their soul plate and frame are built for grinding, according to Spassov and Ricardo Lino.

What wheels should my first aggressive skates have?

Most compiled setups use 56-64 mm wheels at 88-92A, for example Chris Haffey 57 mm/88A, Si Coburn 59 mm/90A and Stephen Babcock 60 mm/90A. Bryce Orfield prefers the hardest wheels for durability; Gabriel Talamantes goes softer in the park.

Flat or anti-rocker for a beginner?

The bladers split: Babcock, Burston, Werbeski, Orfield and Spassov ride anti-rocker; Przybylik, Coburn, Salaman, Crofts and Lino ride flat. Spassov and Lino tell beginners to try both, and call anti-rocker easier for learning grinds.

How do I choose the boot?

For the shape of your foot first: Dominic Bruce says the right boot spares long-term pain, and Andrew DiPaolo and Hazen Bell chose their boots for wide feet. For size, follow the brand chart, says the skater behind this site, and do the fastenings up tight.

What protective gear do I need for aggressive skating?

Helmet, wrist guards, elbow pads, knee pads and shin guards, which Bobi Spassov wore for his first six years; he calls shin guards super important for learning grinds. Ricardo Lino adds padded shorts.
